If a pipe bursts in your Burien home, shut off the main water valve first. Then turn off power to any wet area, open the lowest faucet to drain the lines and move valuables away from the water. Take photos for insurance and call a plumber to repair the pipe. Acting in the first few minutes can save thousands in water damage.

A burst pipe can release several gallons a minute. In a short time that soaks drywall, subfloor and insulation. Most bursts in Burien happen after a cold snap, when a frozen section thaws and the crack starts to leak. Others come from old galvanized pipe that rusts through, a failed supply hose or high water pressure. The cause matters later. Right now, stopping the water is the only goal.

## Step by step: what to do in the first 10 minutes

1. **Shut off the main water valve.** Turn it clockwise until it stops. If it is a lever valve, turn the handle so it sits across the pipe.
2. **Turn off electricity to wet areas.** If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, switch off those breakers. Do not stand in water to reach the panel. If you cannot reach it safely, wait for help.
3. **Shut off the water heater.** Set a gas heater to pilot or off. Turn off the breaker for an electric heater. This protects the tank once the water is off.
4. **Open faucets to drain the lines.** Open the lowest faucet in the house and a tub faucet. This drains the water still in the pipes and relieves pressure.
5. **Move valuables and soak up water.** Use towels, buckets and a wet vacuum. Lift rugs and furniture off wet floors.
6. **Take photos and video.** Capture the burst pipe, the water and damaged items before cleanup. Your insurer will want them.
7. **Call a plumber.** A plumber can cut out the damaged section and restore water, often the same day.

## Where burst pipes happen most in Burien homes

| Location | Common cause |
| --- | --- |
| Crawl space supply lines | Freezing during cold snaps |
| Outdoor faucet pipe inside the wall | Hose left connected in winter |
| Old galvanized lines | Rust from the inside out |
| Washing machine and toilet hoses | Worn rubber supply hoses |
| Pipes in outside walls | Freezing and poor insulation |

Many local homes from the 1940s to 1960s still have galvanized steel pipe. It weakens over time, so a burst in one spot often means other sections are close behind.

## Should you call insurance or a plumber first?

Call the plumber first if water is still leaking or you cannot stop it. Stopping the water limits damage, and most policies expect you to prevent further loss. Then call your insurer. Sudden bursts are often covered, while slow leaks from wear usually are not. Keep every receipt and photo.

## How to prevent the next burst

- Insulate pipes in the crawl space, garage and outside walls.
- Disconnect hoses before freezing weather.
- Replace rubber washer and toilet supply hoses with braided steel every few years.
- Keep the heat at 55°F or higher in winter.
- Plan to replace old galvanized pipe before it fails.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long can a burst pipe go before damage gets serious?
Water can soak drywall and subfloor within an hour. Mold can start to grow within 24 to 48 hours if materials stay wet. Shut the water off right away and start drying the area.

### Can I fix a burst pipe myself?
A repair clamp or push fitting can stop a small leak for a short time. A permanent fix usually means cutting out the damaged section. If the pipe is galvanized or inside a wall, call a plumber.

### Will my water still work after the main is shut off?
No. Every faucet and toilet will be off until the pipe is repaired. A plumber can often isolate the damaged line and turn the rest of the house back on.

### Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?
Many policies cover sudden and accidental bursts, including freeze damage, if the home was heated. Damage from a slow leak or neglect is often excluded. Check your policy and call your agent.
