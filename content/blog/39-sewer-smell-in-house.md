A sewer smell in the house usually comes from a dry drain trap, a failed toilet wax ring, a blocked or broken vent pipe, a cracked drain line or a partial sewer backup. Start by running water in every drain you rarely use. If the smell stays, check around toilets for loose bases and listen for gurgling drains. Persistent smells need a plumber to test the system.

Sewer gas contains hydrogen sulfide, which smells like rotten eggs, plus methane and other gases. In normal amounts it is unpleasant more than dangerous, but strong or ongoing smells should not be ignored. Older Burien homes with cast iron drains in crawl spaces and clay side sewers are more likely to develop cracks that let gas escape.

## 7 common causes

1. **Dry P trap.** Every drain has a U shaped trap that holds water to block gas. Guest bathrooms, basement floor drains and laundry sinks dry out when unused.
2. **Failed toilet wax ring.** The seal under the toilet breaks down. You may see a rocking toilet or water at the base.
3. **Blocked vent pipe.** Leaves, nests or ice block the roof vent. Drains gurgle and traps get siphoned dry.
4. **Cracked drain or vent pipe.** Cast iron rusts through, and old joints separate in walls and crawl spaces.
5. **Partial sewer backup.** A clog in the main line lets gas build up and push through traps.
6. **Loose cleanout cap.** A missing or loose cap on an indoor or crawl space cleanout lets gas escape.
7. **Washing machine standpipe.** A dry or improperly vented laundry drain can release odor.

## Where the smell is tells you a lot

| Where it smells | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| One bathroom | Dry trap, wax ring or vent |
| Basement or floor drain | Dry floor drain trap |
| Crawl space | Cracked drain pipe or loose cleanout |
| Whole house after rain | Sewer line or vent problem |
| Laundry room | Standpipe trap or vent |
| Outside near the house | Side sewer leak or cleanout |

## Quick fixes to try

- Run water for 30 seconds in every unused sink, tub and floor drain.
- Add a little mineral oil to rarely used floor drains to slow evaporation.
- Check that toilets are firmly seated and caulked.
- Make sure cleanout caps are tight. See [sewer cleanout location](/blog/sewer-cleanout-location/).

## Signs you need a plumber

- The smell returns after refilling traps.
- Drains gurgle or run slowly. Read [gurgling drains](/blog/gurgling-drains/).
- A toilet rocks or leaks at the base.
- You see wet spots or smell sewage in the crawl space.
- Multiple drains back up.

A plumber can run a smoke test, which pushes harmless smoke into the drain system to show where gas escapes. A camera inspection can find cracks in the side sewer. See [sewer scope inspection](/blog/sewer-scope-inspection/).

## Is sewer gas dangerous?

At low levels it mainly causes odor, headaches and nausea. High concentrations in enclosed spaces are dangerous. If the smell is strong and you feel unwell, ventilate the area and leave. If you smell natural gas rather than sewage, leave the house and call Puget Sound Energy or 911.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my house smell like sewer only sometimes?
Traps can dry out, wind can push air down vents, and heavy rain can change pressure in the sewer. If it happens often, have the system checked.

### Can a sewer smell come from a shower drain?
Yes. A dry or poorly vented shower trap, or buildup of hair and soap, can produce odors.

### How do I know if my wax ring is bad?
A rocking toilet, water around the base or a smell near the toilet are common signs.

### Does a sewer smell mean a broken pipe?
Not always. Dry traps are the most common cause. If the smell continues after refilling traps, a cracked pipe or vent problem is possible.
