Low water pressure in a house is most often caused by clogged aerators or shower heads, a partly closed shutoff valve, a failing pressure reducing valve, a hidden leak, or corroded galvanized pipe. If only one fixture is weak, the problem is at that fixture. If the whole house is weak, the cause is in the main line, the pressure valve or the pipe system itself.

In older Burien homes, the most common whole house cause is galvanized steel pipe. Rust builds up inside the pipe for decades and narrows it, much like plaque in an artery. Pressure from the street can be fine, but very little gets through the pipe. Homes from the 1940s to 1960s in Burien, White Center and Boulevard Park often have this problem.

## Start by narrowing it down

| What you notice | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| One faucet or shower is weak | Clogged aerator, shower head or cartridge |
| Only hot water is weak | Water heater sediment or corroded hot lines |
| Whole house is weak | Main valve, pressure reducing valve, leak or old pipe |
| Pressure dropped suddenly | Leak, valve problem or a district issue |
| Weak upstairs only | Undersized or corroded pipe |

## Common causes and fixes

1. **Clogged aerators and shower heads.** Unscrew the aerator and soak it in vinegar. Clean the shower head the same way.
2. **Partly closed main valve.** After a repair, the valve may not be fully open. Open it all the way.
3. **Failing pressure reducing valve.** Many homes have a PRV near the main line. When it fails, pressure can drop or spike. See [pressure reducing valves](/blog/pressure-reducing-valve/).
4. **Hidden leak.** Water escaping before it reaches fixtures lowers pressure. Check the meter as shown in [signs of a hidden water leak](/blog/signs-of-hidden-water-leak/).
5. **Galvanized pipe buildup.** Rust narrows the pipe. The only lasting fix is replacing it. Read [galvanized pipes in older homes](/blog/galvanized-pipes-older-homes/).
6. **Water heater sediment.** If only hot water is weak, sediment or a clogged dip tube may be the cause.
7. **District work.** Main breaks or maintenance by your water district can lower pressure for a short time. Burien is served by Highline Water District and King County Water Districts 20, 49 and 125.

## How to test water pressure

Buy a pressure gauge that screws onto a hose bib, about $10 to $20. Close all faucets and attach it to the hose bib nearest the main.

| Reading | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Under 40 psi | Low |
| 40 to 80 psi | Normal range |
| Over 80 psi | Too high, strains pipes and fixtures |

If the gauge shows normal pressure at the hose bib but fixtures inside are weak, the problem is inside the house, often old pipe.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my water pressure low all of a sudden?
A sudden drop often means a leak, a valve that was bumped closed, a failing pressure reducing valve or work on the water main. Check with neighbors to see if they are affected.

### Can low water pressure be fixed without repiping?
Often yes, if the cause is an aerator, valve or regulator. If old galvanized pipe is the cause, replacing the pipe is the only lasting fix.

### What is normal water pressure for a house?
Most homes run best between 40 and 80 psi. Around 50 to 60 psi is ideal.

### Why is my hot water pressure low but cold is fine?
Sediment in the water heater, a clogged dip tube or corroded hot water lines usually cause this. Hot lines in galvanized homes often corrode faster.
