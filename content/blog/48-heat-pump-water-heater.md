A heat pump water heater is an electric water heater that moves heat from the surrounding air into the tank instead of making heat with resistance elements. It can use less than half the electricity of a standard electric tank. For many Burien homes with electric water heaters, it is worth it, especially with utility rebates. It needs enough space, a drain for condensate and a location that stays above about 40°F.

Washington has been moving toward efficient electric equipment, and heat pump water heaters are a big part of that. Puget Sound Energy has offered rebates on qualifying models, and some programs target income qualified households. The units cost more upfront than a standard tank, but lower power bills pay back part of the difference over time.

## How a heat pump water heater works

A small compressor and fan on top of the tank pull warm air across a coil. A refrigerant absorbs that heat and transfers it into the water. The cooler, drier air blows out. Most units also have backup electric elements for times of high demand.

## Pros and cons

| Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- |
| Uses much less electricity than a standard electric tank | Higher upfront cost |
| Rebates often available | Needs space and air volume around it |
| Long tank warranty on many models | Makes a low fan hum |
| Dehumidifies the space it sits in | Cools the room it sits in |
| Works with existing 240 volt circuit in most cases | Needs a condensate drain |

## Is your home a good fit?

- **Location.** Garages, basements and large utility rooms work well. Small closets may need vent ducting or a louvered door.
- **Temperature.** The space should stay above about 40°F most of the time. Many Burien garages qualify.
- **Size.** These units are taller than standard tanks. Measure the ceiling height.
- **Drain.** A floor drain, laundry drain or condensate pump is needed.
- **Electrical.** Most replace a standard electric heater on the same circuit. Some 120 volt models plug into a standard outlet.

## Heat pump vs tankless

Tankless gas heaters give endless hot water but need gas lines and venting. Heat pump heaters are tanks, so they hold a limited supply, but they need no gas or flue. For an electric home, a heat pump model is usually the simpler upgrade. See [tankless water heater pros and cons](/blog/tankless-water-heater-pros-cons/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do heat pump water heaters work in winter?
Yes, as long as the space stays above about 40°F. In colder spaces, they switch to backup electric heat, which uses more power.

### Are heat pump water heaters noisy?
They make a low fan and compressor hum, similar to a dehumidifier. Most people do not notice it in a garage or basement.

### How long do heat pump water heaters last?
Most are expected to last 10 to 15 years with basic care, such as cleaning the air filter.

### Can I put a heat pump water heater in a closet?
Only if the closet is large enough or vented. Many models need around 700 cubic feet of air or ducting.
