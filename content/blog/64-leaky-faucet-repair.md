Leaky faucet repair starts with finding the faucet type and the worn part. Compression faucets with two handles usually need new washers. Single handle faucets usually need a new cartridge, O rings or ceramic disc seals. Turn off the water under the sink, plug the drain, take the handle apart and match the old part at a hardware store. If the faucet body is corroded or very old, replacing it is often the better choice.

A faucet that drips once a second wastes thousands of gallons a year. Beyond the water, a constant drip leaves stains, wears out the valve seat and can hide a bigger problem. In older Burien homes, faucets may also be connected to galvanized supply lines and old shutoff valves that need attention when you make the repair.

## Identify your faucet type

| Faucet type | How to recognize it | Common fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compression | Two handles, you tighten to stop the flow | Replace rubber washers and seats |
| Cartridge | One or two handles, smooth action | Replace the cartridge |
| Ball | Single handle on a rounded cap, common in kitchens | Replace the ball, seals and springs |
| Ceramic disc | Single lever over a wide cylinder | Replace the disc cartridge or seals |

## Where is it leaking?

- **From the spout.** A worn washer, cartridge or seal inside the valve.
- **Around the handle.** A worn O ring or packing.
- **Under the sink.** A loose supply line or connection. See [water under the kitchen sink](/blog/water-under-kitchen-sink/).
- **From the base.** Worn O rings in a kitchen faucet spout.

## Step by step: basic repair

1. **Shut off the water** at the valves under the sink. Open the faucet to drain it.
2. **Plug the drain** so small parts do not fall in.
3. **Remove the handle.** Pry off the cap and remove the screw.
4. **Remove the valve.** Unscrew the retaining nut or clip and pull out the cartridge or stem.
5. **Take the old part to the store** to get an exact match.
6. **Install the new part** and reassemble.
7. **Turn the water on slowly** and test.

## When to replace the faucet

- The faucet body is cracked or badly corroded.
- Parts are no longer available.
- It still leaks after new parts.
- You are updating the kitchen or bath anyway.

## Check the shutoff valves too

Old multi turn shutoff valves under sinks often seize or leak when used. If yours will not close fully, replace it with a quarter turn valve during the repair.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my faucet still drip after I replaced the washer?
The valve seat may be worn or pitted. Replace or resurface the seat, or replace the cartridge if the faucet uses one.

### How much water does a dripping faucet waste?
A drip of one per second can waste thousands of gallons a year.

### Should I repair or replace a leaky faucet?
Repair if the faucet is in good shape and parts are available. Replace if it is old, corroded or you want a new style.

### Do I need to turn off the main water to fix a faucet?
No, if the shutoff valves under the sink work. If they do not, turn off the main.
