A ceiling water stain is caused by either a plumbing leak or a roof or weather leak. If the stain sits under a bathroom, kitchen or laundry room, and grows when fixtures are used, it is likely plumbing. If it appears or grows after rain, sits near an exterior wall or below the attic, it is likely the roof, flashing or gutters. In Burien, rainy months make both common.

Plumbing leaks above ceilings often come from a failed toilet wax ring, a leaking shower valve or drain, a supply line in the floor framing or a condensation drip from a pipe. Roof leaks show up during storms. Knowing which one you have saves time, because you call the right trade the first time.

## Plumbing leak or roof leak?

| Clue | Plumbing leak | Roof or weather leak |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Location | Under a bathroom, kitchen or laundry | Near exterior walls, below attic, near chimney or skylight |
| Timing | After showers, flushes or appliance use | During or after rain |
| Growth | Grows with water use | Grows with storms, dries in dry weather |
| Smell | May smell like sewage if a drain leaks | Musty, earthy |
| Upper floor | Leak source directly above | Water may travel along rafters |

## What to do right away

1. **Protect the room.** Move furniture and electronics. Put down a tarp and a bucket.
2. **Check for sagging.** If the ceiling bulges with water, poke a small hole at the lowest point with a screwdriver to drain it into a bucket. This prevents a larger collapse.
3. **Turn off power** to lights in the wet area.
4. **Stop using fixtures above** the stain if you suspect plumbing.
5. **Shut off the water** if the leak is active and you cannot find the fixture. See [main water shutoff valve](/blog/main-water-shutoff-valve/).

## Common plumbing sources above a ceiling

- **Toilet wax ring.** Water seeps from the base when flushed.
- **Shower or tub drain.** Leaks during showers.
- **Shower valve.** Leaks inside the wall when the shower runs.
- **Tub overflow gasket.** Leaks when the tub is filled high.
- **Supply line.** Leaks all the time, not just during use.
- **Condensation.** Cold pipes sweat in humid weather and drip.

## How to test

Run each fixture above the stain one at a time for several minutes. Start with the shower, then the tub, then flush the toilet several times. Check the stain after each test. If the stain grows during one test, you have found the likely source.

## Repairing the leak and the ceiling

Fix the leak first, then let the area dry completely before repairing drywall. Painting over a wet stain will not last. A plumber can often repair from the room above or through a small ceiling opening. Leak repair behind drywall usually costs $400 to $1,200 plus the wall patch.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a ceiling stain be from condensation?
Yes. Cold water pipes or ducts can sweat in humid weather and drip onto the ceiling. Insulating the pipe usually solves it.

### Should I paint over a water stain?
Only after the leak is fixed and the area is fully dry. Use a stain blocking primer first.

### Is a ceiling leak an emergency?
If water is dripping, the ceiling is sagging or the stain is near lights, yes. A dry old stain is not urgent but should be investigated.

### Will homeowners insurance cover a ceiling leak?
Sudden plumbing leaks are often covered. Slow leaks and roof wear may not be. Check your policy and take photos.
