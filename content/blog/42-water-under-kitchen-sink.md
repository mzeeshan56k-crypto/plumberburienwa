Water under the kitchen sink usually comes from a loose or worn supply line, a leaking P trap or drain connection, a faulty faucet base or sprayer hose, the dishwasher drain or supply line, or a garbage disposal leak. Dry everything, then run water while you watch each connection with a flashlight. Most of these leaks are simple to fix, but a slow leak left alone can rot the cabinet floor.

Kitchen sink cabinets are full of connections. Cold and hot supply lines, the faucet hoses, the drain trap, the disposal, the dishwasher hose and sometimes a water filter all share one small space. Because the cabinet is often packed with cleaning supplies, a slow drip can go unnoticed until the cabinet floor is stained or soft.

## Find the source

1. Empty the cabinet and dry every surface and pipe.
2. Put dry paper towels on the cabinet floor.
3. Check the supply lines with the water off, then on.
4. Run the faucet with the drain open and watch the trap and drain pipes.
5. Fill the sink, then release it, to put pressure on the drain connections.
6. Run the dishwasher and the disposal and check again.

## Common sources and fixes

| Source | Signs | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supply line or shutoff valve | Drip with the faucet off | Tighten or replace the line or valve |
| Faucet hoses or base | Leak only when the faucet runs | Tighten or replace the faucet hoses |
| Sprayer hose | Leak when using the sprayer | Replace the sprayer hose |
| P trap or slip nuts | Leak when draining | Tighten nuts, replace washers or the trap |
| Sink strainer | Leak around the drain basket | Reseal with plumber's putty |
| Dishwasher hose | Leak during a cycle | Tighten or replace the hose |
| Garbage disposal | Leak from the unit body | Replace the disposal |
| Water filter or ice maker line | Drip at the fitting | Replace the fitting |

## Quick fixes you can do

- **Tighten slip nuts** on the trap by hand, then a quarter turn with pliers.
- **Replace braided supply lines.** They are inexpensive and screw on by hand.
- **Reseal the sink strainer** if water leaks around the drain basket.

## When it is a bigger problem

- The shutoff valve leaks or will not close.
- The leak comes from inside the wall.
- The disposal body leaks.
- The cabinet floor is soft or moldy.
- Water reaches the floor or the room below.

If the disposal is the problem, read [garbage disposal dos and don'ts](/blog/garbage-disposal-dos-and-donts/) to decide on repair or replacement. If the sink is also slow, see [how to fix a clogged kitchen sink](/blog/clogged-kitchen-sink/).

## Prevent leaks

- Check under the sink once a month.
- Replace plastic or rubber supply lines with braided steel every 5 to 8 years.
- Do not overload the cabinet so pipes get bumped.
- Consider a small leak sensor under the sink.

If water has been leaking for a while, look for other [signs of a hidden water leak](/blog/signs-of-hidden-water-leak/). For a leak you cannot stop, close the [main water shutoff valve](/blog/main-water-shutoff-valve/) and call a [Burien plumber](/) at (833) 710-2588.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is there water under my sink but no visible leak?
It may be a slow drip that only happens when the faucet or dishwasher runs. Dry everything and test each fixture one at a time.

### Can a leaking faucet cause water under the sink?
Yes. Worn faucet hoses or a loose base nut can drip under the sink, especially when the faucet is on.

### Is a slow drip under the sink urgent?
It is not an emergency, but fix it soon. Slow leaks rot cabinet floors and can lead to mold.

### How often should supply lines be replaced?
Braided steel supply lines last about 5 to 10 years. Replace them when you replace a faucet or if they show rust or bulges.
