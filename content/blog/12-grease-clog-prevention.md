Grease clog prevention comes down to one rule: keep fats, oils and grease out of the drain. Let grease cool in a container and throw it in the trash, wipe pans before washing, and use a sink strainer. Hot water and dish soap do not stop grease from hardening farther down the pipe, where it traps food and builds into a stubborn clog.

Plumbers and sewer districts call it FOG: fats, oils and grease. It is the leading cause of kitchen drain backups and a major cause of sewer overflows. In homes around Burien, FOG builds up inside kitchen branch lines and then in the side sewer. Older galvanized and cast iron drains have rough inside walls, which catch grease faster than smooth plastic pipe.

## Where grease comes from

- Bacon, sausage and ground beef fat
- Cooking oil and frying oil
- Butter, margarine and lard
- Gravy, sauces and salad dressings
- Dairy, like milk and cream, in large amounts
- Meat juices from roasting pans

## Why hot water does not help

Hot water keeps grease liquid for a few feet. Once it reaches cooler pipe, often under the floor or in the crawl space, it hardens and sticks to the walls. Each layer narrows the pipe. Food particles and soap then catch on the grease and form a clog.

## Habits that prevent grease clogs

1. Pour cooled grease into a can or jar with a lid. Throw it out when full.
2. Wipe pans, plates and utensils with a paper towel before washing.
3. Scrape food scraps into the trash or compost.
4. Use a fine mesh strainer in every kitchen drain.
5. Run cold water when using the garbage disposal, never hot.
6. Do not rely on the disposal to handle grease. See [garbage disposal dos and don'ts](/blog/garbage-disposal-dos-and-donts/).

## Signs grease is building up

| Sign | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Kitchen sink drains slower each week | Grease is narrowing the line |
| Bad smell from the drain | Food is trapped in grease buildup |
| Dishwasher water backs up into the sink | Kitchen line is partly blocked |
| Clog returns weeks after clearing | The pipe walls are still coated |

## How plumbers remove grease buildup

A cable machine punches a hole through a grease clog, but much of the buildup stays on the pipe walls. That is why grease clogs often come back. Hydro jetting uses high pressure water to scour the pipe clean. It is the best long term fix for kitchen lines. Learn more in [hydro jetting vs snaking](/blog/hydro-jetting-vs-snaking/).

## Do enzyme cleaners work?

Monthly enzyme or bacteria based drain treatments can help reduce light buildup. They work slowly and will not clear an active clog. They are safer for pipes than harsh chemical cleaners, which our article on [chemical drain cleaners](/blog/chemical-drain-cleaners/) warns against.

## Why it matters beyond your home

Grease that gets past your side sewer ends up in the public sewer main. Sewer districts serving Burien work to keep FOG out of the system because it causes backups and overflows. Restaurants are required to use grease interceptors. Homes rely on good habits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I pour grease down the drain if I run hot water?
No. The grease cools and hardens farther down the pipe. Always throw cooled grease in the trash.

### Does dish soap break down grease in pipes?
Dish soap breaks grease apart for a short time, but it recombines and sticks to the pipe as it cools. Soap is not a substitute for keeping grease out.

### How often should a kitchen line be jetted?
Most homes only need it when clogs repeat. Homes that cook often with oil may benefit from jetting every two to three years.

### Is coconut oil safe to pour down the drain?
No. Coconut oil turns solid at room temperature and is one of the fastest ways to clog a kitchen drain.
