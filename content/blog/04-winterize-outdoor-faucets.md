To winterize outdoor faucets, disconnect the garden hose, shut off the water to the faucet from inside if there is a valve, drain the line, and cover the faucet with an insulated cap. In Burien, do this in October or early November, before the first freeze warning. A split hose bib often leaks inside the wall, where you will not see it until the floor is wet.

Most outdoor faucets in local homes connect to a pipe that runs through the rim joist into the crawl space or garage. When water freezes in that short pipe, it expands and splits the copper or galvanized line. The leak only shows up when you turn the faucet on in spring, or when it thaws after a cold snap. By then water may have soaked the crawl space or the wall cavity.

## What you need

- A bucket and a towel
- An insulated hose bib cover, about $5 to $15
- A flashlight for the crawl space or basement
- A flathead screwdriver or pliers for some shutoff valves

## Step by step: winterize a standard hose bib

1. **Disconnect every hose.** A connected hose traps water in the faucet. Drain the hose and store it.
2. **Find the indoor shutoff.** Look inside, directly behind the outdoor faucet. It may be in the crawl space, basement, garage or a nearby cabinet.
3. **Close the indoor valve.** Turn it clockwise, or turn the lever across the pipe.
4. **Open the outdoor faucet.** Let the trapped water drain out. Leave it open for the winter.
5. **Open the bleeder cap if there is one.** Some indoor valves have a small cap on the side. Place a bucket under it, open it to drain the last water, then close it again.
6. **Install an insulated cover.** Even after draining, a cover adds protection from wind.

## What if there is no indoor shutoff?

Many older Burien homes have hose bibs with no separate valve. In that case:

- Disconnect the hose and close the faucet tightly.
- Install a thick insulated cover.
- Insulate the pipe just inside the wall, in the crawl space or garage.
- Consider a frost free hose bib before next winter.

## Standard vs frost free hose bibs

| Feature | Standard hose bib | Frost free hose bib |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the valve seals | At the outside wall | 6 to 12 inches inside the wall |
| Freeze risk | High if water is left inside | Low if the hose is removed |
| Needs indoor shutoff | Yes, for best protection | Not required |
| Installed cost | $150 to $300 | $250 to $450 |

A frost free hose bib still freezes if you leave a hose connected, because water cannot drain. Always remove the hose.

## Sprinkler systems and outdoor kitchens

If you have an irrigation system, shut it off at its own valve and have it blown out with compressed air. Outdoor sinks and showers need the same drain down as a hose bib.

## Signs your hose bib already froze

- Water drips inside the wall or crawl space when the faucet is on.
- The faucet sprays from the handle or the body.
- Low flow from the spout in spring.
- A damp spot on the wall or ceiling near the faucet.

A split line inside the wall should be repaired right away. If water is leaking into the house, shut off the main valve. Our guide to [finding your main water shutoff valve](/blog/main-water-shutoff-valve/) shows where to look.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should I winterize outdoor faucets in Burien?
Aim for late October or early November. Hard freezes in the Burien area are most common from December through February, but early cold snaps happen.

### Do frost free hose bibs need to be winterized?
They need the hose removed. With the hose off, the water drains from the long valve body and the faucet is protected. An insulated cover adds extra protection in very cold weather.

### Can I leave the outdoor faucet dripping instead?
Dripping an outdoor faucet wastes water and can still freeze at the spout. Draining the line is more reliable.

### How do I know if my hose bib is frost free?
A frost free hose bib usually has a longer body that extends into the wall and an anti siphon cap on top. If the shutoff sits right at the outside wall, it is a standard bib.
