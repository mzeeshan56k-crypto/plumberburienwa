The most common slab leak signs are a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when nothing is on, a water bill that keeps rising, damp or cracked flooring, and a water meter that moves with every fixture off. Slab leaks happen when a water line under a concrete floor develops a pinhole or a failed joint. Most are repaired by rerouting the line overhead rather than breaking up the floor.

Most Burien homes sit over crawl spaces, where leaks are easier to find and fix. Slab construction is more common in some newer homes, additions, townhomes, garages converted to living space and daylight basement levels. In those areas, copper lines buried in or under the slab can corrode over time and start to leak where you cannot see them.

## Warning signs of a slab leak

| Sign | What it suggests |
| --- | --- |
| Warm or hot spot on the floor | Hot water line leak |
| Sound of running water with taps off | Pressurized leak |
| Water meter moves with everything off | Leak somewhere in the system |
| Damp carpet or warped flooring | Water rising through the slab |
| New cracks in tile or concrete | Soil movement from water |
| Mold or musty smell near the floor | Ongoing moisture |
| Low water pressure | Large leak |
| Water heater running constantly | Hot water leaking away |

## How to check for a slab leak

1. Turn off every fixture and appliance.
2. Watch the leak indicator on your water meter.
3. Close the main valve at the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside.
4. Walk the floors barefoot to feel for warm spots.
5. Close the cold supply to the water heater. If the meter stops, the leak is on the hot side.

## How plumbers find slab leaks

Professional leak detection uses:

- Acoustic listening devices that hear water escaping under the floor.
- Thermal cameras that show warm water lines.
- Pressure testing to isolate hot and cold lines.
- Line tracing to map the pipe route.

This pinpoints the leak so only a small area is opened, or none at all. Leak detection usually costs $200 to $600 in the Burien area.

## Repair options

| Option | How it works | Typical Burien area cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spot repair | Break a small section of slab, fix the pipe, patch the concrete | $1,500 to $4,000 |
| Reroute | Abandon the buried line and run a new one through walls or the ceiling | $1,500 to $5,000 |
| Repipe | Replace all lines if leaks keep happening | $8,000 to $20,000 |

Rerouting is often preferred because it avoids breaking concrete and the new line is easier to reach later. If one slab leak has happened, others may follow. Our guide to [pinhole leaks in copper pipes](/blog/pinhole-leaks-copper-pipes/) explains why.

## Act quickly

A slab leak can raise water bills, damage flooring, encourage mold and even affect the soil under the foundation. If you see the signs, shut off the water if the leak is large and call for help. Read [how to find your main water shutoff valve](/blog/main-water-shutoff-valve/) so you are ready.

## Does insurance cover slab leaks?

Many policies cover the cost of accessing the leak and resulting water damage, but not the pipe repair itself. Some exclude long term leaks. Check your policy.

## Frequently asked questions

### What causes slab leaks?
Corrosion of copper lines, abrasion where pipes rub against concrete or gravel, poor original installation and soil movement are the main causes.

### Is a slab leak an emergency?
A large leak or one causing water damage is. A small leak should still be fixed within days to prevent damage and mold.

### Can you fix a slab leak without breaking the floor?
Yes. Rerouting the line through walls or the ceiling avoids breaking the slab. Some leaks can also be repaired from a small access point.

### How long does slab leak repair take?
Most reroutes take one to two days. Spot repairs can take a day, plus time for concrete patching.
