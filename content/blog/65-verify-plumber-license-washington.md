To verify a plumber's license in Washington, use the free Verify tool on the Washington State Department of Labor and Industries website, lni.wa.gov/verify. Search the company to confirm it is a registered contractor with an active bond and insurance. Then ask the plumber for their state plumber certification card and confirm it is current. Both the company and the individual plumber should check out.

In Washington, plumbing is a regulated trade. Individual plumbers must hold a state certificate, such as a journey level or residential specialty certificate, or work as a registered trainee under supervision. Plumbing companies must be registered with L&I and are required to have a certified plumber designated to the business. This protects homeowners from unqualified work and gives you recourse if something goes wrong.

## Step by step: check a plumber with L&I

1. Go to lni.wa.gov/verify.
2. Search by business name, owner name or registration number.
3. Confirm the registration is active.
4. Check that the bond and insurance are current.
5. Look for any infractions, lawsuits against the bond or warnings.
6. Search the individual plumber by name to confirm their certification.

## What to look for

| Check | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Active contractor registration | The business is legally registered |
| Bond | Financial protection if the job is not completed or code is not met |
| Liability insurance | Coverage for damage to your property |
| Plumber certification | The worker is qualified for the type of work |
| Infractions or violations | History of problems |

## Questions to ask before hiring

- Are you registered with Washington L&I?
- Will a certified plumber do the work, or a trainee under supervision?
- Will you pull the permit?
- Is the price firm, and is it in writing?
- What warranty do you offer on parts and labor?
- Do you carry liability insurance?

## Red flags

- No registration number on the truck, quote or website.
- Refuses to pull a permit for work that needs one.
- Asks for full payment up front.
- No written estimate.
- Pressures you to decide immediately.
- Cash only with no receipt.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do plumbers need a license in Washington?
Yes. Individual plumbers need a state certificate, and plumbing businesses must be registered contractors with L&I.

### What is the difference between a journey level and a residential plumber?
A journey level plumber can work on all types of buildings. A residential specialty plumber is certified for single family and small residential work.

### Can a handyman do plumbing in Washington?
Plumbing work generally requires a certified plumber. Handymen are limited in what they can legally do.

### What if I hired an unlicensed plumber?
You may have little protection if the work fails. Contact L&I for guidance and have the work inspected by a licensed plumber.
