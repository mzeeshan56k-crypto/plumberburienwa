A good plumbing checklist before vacation is short and simple. Before leaving home for a trip, shut off the main water valve, set the water heater to vacation or pilot mode, keep the heat at 55°F or higher, and check for leaks under sinks and around toilets. In winter, protect exposed pipes from freezing. These simple steps turn a possible flood into, at worst, a small drip.

The most expensive plumbing calls often come after a trip. A washer hose bursts or a pipe freezes and splits, and water runs for days before anyone comes home. Winter trips carry extra risk in Burien because cold snaps often arrive around the holidays. A house with the heat turned down and no one home is exactly where pipes freeze.

## Checklist: the day before you leave

1. **Check for drips** under every sink and around the base of toilets.
2. **Look at washer hoses.** Replace any with cracks or bulges.
3. **Run the garbage disposal** with cold water to clear food.
4. **Clean the kitchen sink drain** so nothing sits in the trap.
5. **Disconnect garden hoses** in fall and winter.

## Checklist: as you leave

| Step | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Shut off the main water valve | Stops any leak from becoming a flood |
| Set the water heater to vacation or pilot | Saves energy and protects the tank with the water off |
| Leave the heat at 55°F or higher | Prevents frozen pipes |
| Open cabinets on outside walls in winter | Lets heat reach pipes |
| Turn off the washing machine supply valves | Protects against hose failure |
| Leave the sump pump plugged in | It protects the home from rain while you are away |

Never turn off the sump pump. It needs power to protect your home while you are gone. Our [sump pump guide](/blog/sump-pump-guide/) explains how to test it before a trip.

## If you cannot shut off the main water

Some homes need water for irrigation or a fire sprinkler system. If so, close the shutoff valves under each sink and behind each toilet, and turn off the washer valves. Ask a neighbor to check the house every few days.

If you are not sure where your main valve is, read [how to find your main water shutoff valve](/blog/main-water-shutoff-valve/).

## Winter trips: extra freeze protection

- Insulate crawl space and garage pipes before you go.
- Close crawl space vents or cover them.
- Drain outdoor faucets. See [winterize outdoor faucets](/blog/winterize-outdoor-faucets/).
- Consider draining the plumbing system for long trips in very cold weather.
- Ask someone to check the house if a cold snap is forecast.

Our full guide to [preventing frozen pipes](/blog/prevent-frozen-pipes/) covers every step.

## Smart devices that help

Leak sensors placed under sinks, near the water heater and by the washer send an alert to your phone. Automatic shutoff valves close the main line when they detect a leak. Both are worth considering for second homes and frequent travelers.

## When you get home

1. Open the main water valve slowly.
2. Turn the water heater back on.
3. Run each faucet for a minute to clear air.
4. Check under sinks and around the water heater for leaks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I turn off my water when I go on vacation?
Yes, for most homes. It is the single best way to prevent a flood while you are away.

### Should I turn off my water heater for a trip?
Set it to vacation or pilot mode. With the main water off, this protects the tank and saves energy.

### What temperature should I leave my house at in winter?
Keep it at 55°F or higher. Lower temperatures put crawl space and outside wall pipes at risk.

### Do I need someone to check on my house?
For trips longer than a few days in winter, yes. A quick check can catch a frozen pipe before it causes damage.
