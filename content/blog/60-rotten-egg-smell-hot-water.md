A rotten egg smell in hot water is usually caused by a reaction between bacteria in the water heater and the magnesium anode rod, which produces hydrogen sulfide gas. It is more common in heaters that sit unused or are set to a low temperature. Fixes include flushing and disinfecting the tank, raising the temperature for a short time and replacing the anode rod with an aluminum zinc or powered anode.

Before anything else, make sure the smell is in the water and not in the air. Natural gas has a rotten egg odor added so leaks can be detected. If you smell it near the water heater, stove or furnace, and not just when water runs, leave the house and call Puget Sound Energy or 911 from outside.

## Is it the water heater?

| Test | Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smell only in hot water | Yes | Water heater is the source |
| Smell in hot and cold water | Yes | Water supply or drains, not the heater |
| Smell from the drain, not the water | Yes | Dry trap or drain buildup |
| Smell in the air near gas appliances | Yes | Possible gas leak, leave and call |

To test, fill a glass with cold water and step away from the sink to smell it. Then do the same with hot water.

## Why it happens

1. **Bacteria.** Harmless sulfate reducing bacteria can live in the tank.
2. **Magnesium anode rod.** The rod reacts with the bacteria and produces hydrogen sulfide.
3. **Low temperature.** Water kept below about 120°F lets bacteria grow.
4. **Unused heater.** Vacation homes, rentals and guest suites where water sits for weeks.

## How to fix it

**Flush the tank.** Drain and refill to clear sediment where bacteria live. Our guide on [how to flush a water heater](/blog/flush-water-heater/) shows the steps.

**Raise the temperature briefly.** Setting the heater to 140°F for several hours can kill bacteria. Run hot water through each faucet afterward, then return to 120°F. Be careful of scalding, and warn everyone in the house.

**Replace the anode rod.** Swapping a magnesium rod for an aluminum zinc rod or a powered anode is the most lasting fix. Removing the anode entirely is not recommended, because the tank will rust quickly and may void the warranty.

**Disinfect the tank.** A plumber can add a hydrogen peroxide solution to disinfect the tank safely.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is rotten egg smelling hot water safe?
The smell is unpleasant but usually not harmful at the low levels found in water heaters. Fix it to make the water pleasant to use.

### Why did my hot water start smelling after vacation?
Water sitting in the tank lets bacteria grow. Flush the heater and run hot water through every faucet.

### Can I remove the anode rod to stop the smell?
Do not remove it. The tank will corrode and fail much sooner. Replace it with a different anode type instead.

### Does a water softener cause the smell?
It can make it worse, because softened water can speed up anode reactions. An aluminum zinc or powered anode helps.
