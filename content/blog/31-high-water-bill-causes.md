A high water bill is most often caused by a running toilet, a hidden leak in the house or crawl space, a leaking yard service line, an irrigation system problem or a change in how your household uses water. To find the cause, check the water meter with every fixture off. If it moves, you have a leak. Then test toilets, which are the most common culprit.

Burien homes get water from Highline Water District or King County Water Districts 20, 49 or 125, depending on the address. Most bill every one or two months. Because the bill comes after the fact, a leak can run for weeks before you notice the cost. Comparing each bill to the same period last year is the fastest way to catch a problem.

## Common causes of a high water bill

| Cause | How much water it can waste | How to spot it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Running toilet | Hundreds of gallons a day | Hissing or refilling between flushes |
| Leaking flapper, silent | Dozens of gallons a day | Food coloring test |
| Dripping faucet | Several gallons a day | Visible drip |
| Leak in the crawl space | Varies, can be large | Damp insulation, meter moves |
| Yard service line leak | Can be very large | Soggy lawn, meter moves with house valve closed |
| Irrigation leak | Large in summer | Wet spots, broken heads |
| Water softener stuck in regeneration | Large | Constant draining sound |
| Seasonal use | Varies | Garden watering, filling a pool |

## Step 1: Check the meter

1. Turn off all faucets, appliances and irrigation.
2. Wait for toilets to finish filling.
3. Find the meter box near the street and lift the lid.
4. Watch the small leak indicator. If it turns, water is flowing.
5. Close the main valve at the house. If the indicator stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps turning, the leak is in the yard line.

A leak between the meter and the house is the homeowner's to repair in most cases. Our guide to a [leaking water service line](/blog/water-service-line-leak/) explains the fix.

## Step 2: Test every toilet

Put a few drops of food coloring in each toilet tank. Wait 15 minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper leaks. Our guide on [how to check a toilet for leaks](/blog/check-toilet-for-leaks/) covers the fix, and [running toilet fix](/blog/running-toilet-fix/) shows how to replace the parts.

## Step 3: Look for hidden leaks

Check under sinks, around the water heater, behind the washer and in the crawl space. Look for damp spots, stains and musty smells. Our full list is in [signs of a hidden water leak](/blog/signs-of-hidden-water-leak/).

## Step 4: Think about usage

- Did someone move in, or did guests stay?
- Did you water the garden more this season?
- Did you fill a hot tub or pool?
- Is a new appliance using more water than expected?

## Can you get a leak adjustment?

Some water districts offer a one time bill adjustment for leaks that have been repaired. Policies vary. Call your district and ask, and keep the repair invoice as proof.

## When to call a plumber

If the meter shows a leak and you cannot find it, professional leak detection can pinpoint it with listening equipment and thermal imaging. It usually costs $200 to $600, with the repair priced separately. Call a [Burien plumber](/) at (833) 710-2588 for help.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why did my water bill double?
The most common reasons are a running toilet, a hidden leak or a yard line leak. Check the meter with everything off to confirm.

### Can a running toilet really raise my bill that much?
Yes. A toilet that runs constantly can waste hundreds of gallons a day, enough to double a typical bill.

### Who is responsible for a leak before the meter?
The water district usually handles leaks on its side of the meter. Leaks between the meter and the house are usually the homeowner's.

### Will my water district reduce my bill after a leak?
Some offer a one time leak adjustment once the leak is fixed. Call your district and ask about their policy.
