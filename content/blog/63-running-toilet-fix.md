To fix a running toilet, first look inside the tank. If water flows into the overflow tube, lower the float or replace the fill valve. If water leaks from the tank into the bowl, replace the flapper. If the flapper does not seal after a flush, adjust the chain so it has a little slack. These three parts cause almost every running toilet.

A toilet that runs on and off, or hisses all day, can waste hundreds of gallons. It is one of the most common reasons for a high water bill in Burien homes. The good news is that toilet tank parts are inexpensive, easy to find at any hardware store and simple to replace.

## How a toilet tank works

- The **flapper** at the bottom opens when you flush and seals the tank when it drops.
- The **fill valve** refills the tank and stops when the **float** rises.
- The **overflow tube** sends extra water into the bowl if the fill valve does not shut off.
- The **chain** connects the handle to the flapper.

## Find the problem

| What you see | Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Water running into the overflow tube | Float set too high or bad fill valve | Lower the float or replace the fill valve |
| Tank refills every few minutes | Flapper leaking | Replace the flapper |
| Flapper does not close after flush | Chain too short or tangled | Adjust the chain |
| Constant hissing | Fill valve not shutting off | Replace the fill valve |
| Handle sticks down | Handle or chain catching | Adjust or replace the handle |

Use the dye test in [how to check a toilet for leaks](/blog/check-toilet-for-leaks/) to confirm a flapper leak.

## Fix 1: Adjust the chain

Leave about half an inch of slack. Too tight keeps the flapper open. Too loose can get caught under it.

## Fix 2: Lower the float

The water level should sit about an inch below the top of the overflow tube. On a modern fill valve, turn the adjustment screw or slide the float clip down. On an older ball float, bend the rod down slightly.

## Fix 3: Replace the flapper

1. Turn off the water supply valve behind the toilet.
2. Flush to drain the tank.
3. Unhook the old flapper from the pegs on the overflow tube.
4. Take it to the store to match the size, usually 2 or 3 inches.
5. Install the new flapper and connect the chain.
6. Turn the water on and test.

## Fix 4: Replace the fill valve

1. Turn off the water and flush.
2. Sponge out the remaining water.
3. Disconnect the supply line under the tank.
4. Unscrew the locknut and lift out the old fill valve.
5. Install the new valve, set the height and reconnect the supply line.
6. Turn the water on and adjust the water level.

## When to call a plumber

- The shutoff valve behind the toilet will not close or leaks.
- Water leaks at the base of the toilet.
- The tank is cracked.
- Parts are replaced but the toilet still runs.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much water does a running toilet waste?
A toilet running constantly can waste hundreds of gallons a day, enough to double a typical water bill.

### Why does my toilet run every few minutes?
Water is slowly leaking from the tank into the bowl, usually through a worn flapper. The fill valve turns on to refill the tank.

### How much do toilet repair parts cost?
Flappers cost a few dollars. Fill valves cost about $10 to $25. Most repairs cost under $30 in parts.

### Is a running toilet urgent?
Not an emergency, but fix it soon. You can turn off the supply valve behind the toilet until it is repaired.
