Crawl space plumbing problems in Pacific Northwest homes usually involve hidden leaks, frozen pipes, corroded galvanized lines, sagging drain pipes and moisture from rain. Because few homeowners go under the house, these issues can run for months. A quick look twice a year, in fall and spring, catches most problems while they are still small.

Many Burien homes built from the 1940s through the 1970s sit over vented crawl spaces. Water supply lines, drain lines, the main shutoff and sometimes the water heater connections all run underneath. Our wet winters keep crawl spaces damp, and cold snaps chill them to near outdoor temperature. That combination is hard on plumbing.

## Common crawl space plumbing problems

| Problem | What causes it | Warning signs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slow supply line leak | Corroded galvanized pipe, failing joints | Higher water bill, damp insulation |
| Frozen and split pipe | Bare pipe near open vents | No water during a cold snap, leak after thaw |
| Drain line leak | Cracked cast iron, loose joints | Sewer smell, wet soil under bathrooms |
| Sagging drain pipe | Broken or missing hangers | Slow drains, gurgling |
| Standing water | Rain runoff, failed sump pump or plumbing leak | Musty smell, high humidity |
| Fallen insulation | Moisture and age | Exposed pipes, mold |

## How to inspect your crawl space

Bring a bright flashlight, gloves and a mask. Look for:

1. Drips or wet spots along water lines and under bathrooms and the kitchen.
2. Rust, green crust or white mineral trails on pipes.
3. Pipe insulation that has fallen off.
4. Drain pipes that sag or have broken straps.
5. Standing water or wet soil, especially after rain.
6. Signs of animals, which can damage pipe insulation.

If you find a leak, close the main water valve and call a plumber. Our guide to [signs of a hidden water leak](/blog/signs-of-hidden-water-leak/) shows how to confirm a leak with the water meter.

## Protecting crawl space pipes from freezing

- Insulate supply lines with foam sleeves.
- Block or close vents during the coldest weeks.
- Seal gaps around pipes at the rim joist.
- Do not let heat tape touch insulation unless it is rated for that use.

Our guide on [how to prevent frozen pipes](/blog/prevent-frozen-pipes/) covers every step for local homes.

## Moisture and drainage

Rain is the biggest moisture source in most local crawl spaces. Clean gutters, extend downspouts away from the foundation and make sure the ground slopes away from the house. If water collects under the house, a sump pump may be needed. See our [sump pump guide](/blog/sump-pump-guide/). A vapor barrier on the soil also helps keep humidity down.

## When to call a plumber

- Any active leak.
- Sewer smell under the house.
- Drain pipes that are sagging or separated.
- Corroded galvanized or cast iron pipe.
- Standing water you cannot trace to rain.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I check my crawl space plumbing?
Twice a year is a good habit, in fall before winter and in spring after the rainy season.

### Is water in the crawl space a plumbing problem?
Sometimes. Most crawl space water comes from rain and poor drainage, but a leaking supply or drain line can also cause it. If the water is there in dry weather, suspect plumbing.

### Should crawl space vents be closed in winter?
Closing or blocking them during freezing weather helps protect pipes. Reopen them in spring to let moisture escape, unless your crawl space is sealed and conditioned.

### Can a plumber repair pipes in a tight crawl space?
Yes. Plumbers work in crawl spaces every day. Very low clearance can add time, but most repairs are routine.
