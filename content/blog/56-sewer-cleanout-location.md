A sewer cleanout is a capped pipe that gives direct access to your main drain line or side sewer. Plumbers use it to clear clogs and run camera inspections without pulling a toilet or going through the roof. In Burien homes, the cleanout is usually outside near the foundation on the side facing the street, in the crawl space, or in a basement or garage floor. Many older homes have no cleanout at all.

When the main line clogs, the cleanout is the fastest way in. It lets a plumber send a cable or camera straight toward the street. It also gives backed up water a place to escape outside rather than coming up through the lowest drain inside. For older Burien homes with clay side sewers and roots, a good cleanout makes every future cleaning faster and cheaper.

## What a cleanout looks like

- A short pipe, usually 3 or 4 inches across, sticking up from the ground or floor.
- A threaded cap, often with a square nub or a slot on top.
- Made of PVC, ABS or cast iron, depending on age.
- Sometimes a two way cleanout with two caps, giving access toward the house and toward the street.

## Where to look in a Burien home

| Location | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Outside near the foundation | Pipe stub in a planting bed or near the front corner of the house |
| Between the house and the street | In the lawn along the sewer path, sometimes in a small box |
| Crawl space | On the main drain line where it exits the foundation |
| Basement or garage floor | Flush cap in the concrete |
| Near a bathroom | Behind an access panel in some homes |

Tip: Find where your sewer line leaves the house. It usually runs toward the street in a straight line. Your sewer district may also have a record of the side sewer route.

## Why the cleanout matters

1. **Faster clog clearing.** Direct access to the main line.
2. **Lower cost.** No need to pull a toilet or climb on the roof.
3. **Camera inspections.** Easy entry for a sewer scope. See [sewer scope inspection](/blog/sewer-scope-inspection/).
4. **Backup relief.** An outdoor cleanout can let sewage escape outside instead of into the house during a severe clog.

## When to add a cleanout

If your home has no cleanout and you have had a main line clog, adding one is a smart upgrade. It is often done during a [side sewer repair](/blog/side-sewer-guide-burien/) or after a root clearing. Costs vary with depth and location, but it pays for itself over a few cleanings, especially if [tree roots in your sewer line](/blog/tree-roots-in-sewer-line/) need regular cutting.

## Cleanout care tips

- Keep the cap tight. A loose or missing cap lets in debris, rainwater and pests, and can let out sewer gas.
- Do not bury it under landscaping.
- Mark its location so you can find it quickly.
- Never use it as a drain for rainwater or sump discharge.

If you notice a sewer smell near a cleanout, check the cap first. See [sewer smell in the house](/blog/sewer-smell-in-house/) for other causes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I open the sewer cleanout myself?
Yes, carefully, if there is no backup. If sewage is backing up, the line may be under pressure. Open it slowly, stand to the side and expect water to flow out.

### What if my house does not have a cleanout?
A plumber can clear the line through a toilet flange or roof vent. Adding a cleanout makes future work easier.

### Is the sewer cleanout the homeowner's responsibility?
Yes, in most cases. It is part of the side sewer, which the homeowner maintains.

### How do I find a buried cleanout?
Follow the line from where it leaves the house toward the street. A plumber can also locate it with a camera and locator.
