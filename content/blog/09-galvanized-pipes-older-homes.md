Galvanized pipes are steel water pipes coated with zinc. They were standard in homes built from the early 1900s through the 1960s, including many Burien homes from the post war building boom. Over 40 to 60 years the zinc wears away and the steel rusts from the inside. That causes low water pressure, brown water and pinhole leaks. Most homes with galvanized pipe eventually need a partial or whole house repipe.

Many neighborhoods in Burien, White Center and Boulevard Park were built in the 1940s and 1950s. Some of those homes still have original galvanized supply lines, while others have a mix of copper and steel after partial repairs. The mix itself can cause trouble, because joining copper directly to galvanized steel speeds up corrosion at the connection.

## How to tell if you have galvanized pipe

| Pipe | How to spot it | Typical lifespan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Galvanized steel | Dull gray, threaded joints, a magnet sticks | 40 to 60 years |
| Copper | Reddish brown, soldered joints | 50 to 70 years |
| PEX | Red, blue or white plastic tubing | 40 years or more expected |
| CPVC | Cream colored rigid plastic | 30 to 50 years |

Check the pipe where it enters the house, near the water heater and in the crawl space. Scratch it lightly with a key. Galvanized shows a silver gray color. A strong magnet sticks to steel but not to copper.

## Problems galvanized pipe causes

- **Low water pressure.** Rust builds up inside and narrows the pipe. It often starts in the hot side first.
- **Brown or rusty water.** Especially after the water sits overnight.
- **Pinhole leaks.** The pipe rusts through, often at threaded joints.
- **Uneven pressure.** One fixture is weak while others are fine.
- **Lead risk.** Older galvanized pipe can collect lead from upstream sources in some systems. If you are concerned, test your water.

## Repair or repipe?

| Factor | Repair | Repipe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number of leaks in the last 2 years | One | Two or more |
| Water pressure | Normal | Low throughout |
| Pipe type | Copper or PEX in good shape | Galvanized or failing copper |
| Home plans | Selling soon | Staying long term |

A single leak in an accessible spot can be repaired by replacing that section. If the pipe is failing throughout the house, patching one section at a time costs more over the years than a planned repipe.

## Does galvanized pipe affect home sales or insurance?

Home inspectors often flag galvanized pipe. Some insurers ask about it or limit coverage on older plumbing. Buyers may ask for a credit. Replacing it before you sell can remove that issue.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is galvanized pipe dangerous?
Galvanized pipe itself is not toxic, but rust can affect water quality and old pipe can hold lead from other sources. If your home has original galvanized pipe, testing the water is a good idea.

### How long does galvanized pipe last?
About 40 to 60 years. Most galvanized pipe in Burien homes is now past that range.

### Can I replace only part of my galvanized pipe?
Yes. Many homeowners replace the worst sections first, such as the main line and hot water lines. Use a proper dielectric fitting where new pipe meets old steel.

### How long does a repipe take?
Most whole house repipes take two to five days, with water restored each evening. The plumber opens small access holes and patches them when the job is done.
