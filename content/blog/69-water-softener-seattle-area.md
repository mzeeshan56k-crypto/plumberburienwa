Most homes in Burien and the Seattle area do not need a water softener. The regional water supply, which comes largely from the protected Cedar River and Tolt River watersheds, is naturally soft, with low levels of calcium and magnesium. A softener adds cost and maintenance without much benefit. If you have taste, odor or sediment concerns, a filter is usually a better fit than a softener.

Water hardness is a big issue in many parts of the country, where minerals leave scale on fixtures and shorten water heater life. People moving to the Puget Sound area often expect the same. Burien homes get water from Highline Water District or King County Water Districts 20, 49 and 125. Much of that water comes from the Seattle regional system, and some districts blend in their own well water.

## What hard water and soft water mean

| Hardness level | Grains per gallon | What you notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Soft | Under 1 | Little or no scale |
| Slightly hard | 1 to 3.5 | Minimal scale |
| Moderately hard | 3.5 to 7 | Some spotting and scale |
| Hard | 7 to 10.5 | Scale on fixtures, soap does not lather well |
| Very hard | Over 10.5 | Heavy scale, appliance problems |

Seattle regional water typically falls in the soft range. Water from district wells can be different, so check your district's annual water quality report for your exact numbers.

## When a softener might make sense

- Your home uses a private well with hard water.
- Your district's water quality report shows hardness in the hard range.
- You see heavy white scale on fixtures and in kettles.

## Better options for common concerns

| Concern | Better fix |
| --- | --- |
| Chlorine taste or smell | Carbon filter at the sink or whole house |
| Sediment or brown water | Sediment filter, and check your pipes |
| Rotten egg smell in hot water | Anode rod and water heater fixes |
| Worry about lead | Test the water, filter certified for lead |

Brown water is usually a pipe or water heater issue, not hardness. See [brown or rusty tap water](/blog/brown-rusty-tap-water/). For a sulfur smell, read [rotten egg smell in hot water](/blog/rotten-egg-smell-hot-water/).

## Soft water and your water heater

Soft water is easier on tanks because less mineral scale forms. But very soft water can wear the anode rod faster. Have the anode checked every few years, as described in [water heater lifespan](/blog/water-heater-lifespan/).

## Test before you buy

1. Read your water district's annual water quality report.
2. Use an inexpensive hardness test strip.
3. For lead or other concerns, use a state accredited lab.

## If you already have a softener

Many homes in the area came with softeners installed by previous owners. If your water is already soft, the softener may be using salt and water for little benefit. A plumber can bypass or remove it. If you keep it, make sure it is not stuck in a regeneration cycle, which can raise your water bill. See [why is my water bill so high](/blog/high-water-bill-causes/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Burien water hard or soft?
Most Burien homes receive soft water from the Seattle regional supply. Check your district's water quality report to confirm for your address.

### Does soft water damage pipes?
No. Soft water from a public supply is treated to protect pipes.

### Should I remove my existing water softener?
If your water is already soft, the softener may not be needed. A plumber can bypass it.

### What filter is best for Seattle area tap water?
A carbon filter improves taste and removes chlorine. Choose a filter certified for the contaminants you want to remove.
