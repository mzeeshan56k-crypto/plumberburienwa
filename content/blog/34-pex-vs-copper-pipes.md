In the PEX vs copper pipes comparison, PEX costs less, installs faster with fewer fittings and handles freezing better, while copper has a longer track record, resists UV light and is fully recyclable. For most whole house repipes in Burien, PEX is the more affordable choice. Copper makes sense when you want a proven metal system or the pipe will be exposed.

Many older Burien homes need new water lines because their original galvanized steel is failing. When you repipe, the material choice affects the price, how many walls are opened and how the system performs for decades. Both PEX and copper meet Washington plumbing code when installed correctly.

## Side by side comparison

| Factor | PEX | Copper |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material | Flexible cross linked plastic | Rigid metal |
| Whole house repipe cost | $8,000 to $16,000 | $12,000 to $20,000 |
| Install time | Faster, long runs with few fittings | Slower, many soldered joints |
| Freeze resistance | Can expand and often survives a freeze | Can split when frozen |
| Lifespan | 40 years or more expected | 50 to 70 years |
| Sunlight | Must be protected from UV | Not affected |
| Noise | Quieter, less water hammer | Can transmit noise |
| Corrosion | Does not corrode | Can develop pinholes in some water |

## Benefits of PEX

- Lower cost for material and labor.
- Flexible pipe bends around corners, so fewer fittings and fewer leak points.
- Fewer and smaller access holes in walls.
- Better freeze tolerance, useful in crawl spaces.
- Quieter operation.
- Home run manifold systems let you shut off each fixture from one place.

## Benefits of copper

- Decades of proven performance.
- Not affected by sunlight, so it works for exposed runs.
- Rigid and durable against rodents and physical damage.
- Recyclable and holds value.
- Many buyers recognize it as premium.

## Concerns with each

**PEX:** must be kept out of direct sunlight, can be chewed by rodents in crawl spaces, and needs proper support and fittings. Use quality brands and certified fittings.

**Copper:** costs more, takes longer to install, and can develop pinhole leaks over time. Our guide to [pinhole leaks in copper pipes](/blog/pinhole-leaks-copper-pipes/) explains why.

## Which is better for older Burien homes?

Most local homes have crawl spaces, which make PEX routing simple and keep costs down. PEX also handles the occasional cold snap better. Copper is still a solid choice for owners who prefer metal or have exposed runs. Many plumbers use a mix: copper near the water heater and PEX for the long runs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is PEX safe for drinking water?
Yes. PEX used for water supply is certified for drinking water. Choose products that meet NSF standards.

### Does PEX last as long as copper?
Copper has a longer track record. PEX is expected to last 40 years or more, and it has been used widely in homes for decades.

### Can PEX be connected to existing copper or galvanized pipe?
Yes, with the right transition fittings. Connections to galvanized steel should use proper fittings to prevent corrosion.

### Will PEX lower the value of my home?
No. PEX is standard in new construction and accepted by inspectors. Buyers mainly care that old galvanized or polybutylene pipe is gone.
