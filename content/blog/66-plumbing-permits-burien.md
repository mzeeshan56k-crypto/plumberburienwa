Plumbing permits in Burien are generally required for installing or replacing a water heater, repiping, adding or moving fixtures, gas piping and side sewer repairs or connections. Simple repairs, like fixing a leaky faucet, unclogging a drain or replacing a toilet flapper, usually do not need a permit. A licensed plumber normally pulls the permit and schedules the inspection as part of the job.

Permits can feel like paperwork, but they exist for good reasons. An inspection confirms that a gas water heater is vented safely, that new pipes are tested for leaks and that a side sewer connection will not fail. Permitted work also helps when you sell, because buyers and inspectors often ask for permit records on major plumbing work.

## Jobs that usually need a permit

| Project | Permit usually required? |
| --- | --- |
| Water heater replacement | Yes |
| Tankless or heat pump water heater install | Yes |
| Whole house or partial repipe | Yes |
| Adding a bathroom or moving fixtures | Yes |
| Gas piping for appliances | Yes |
| Side sewer repair or replacement | Yes, with sewer district inspection |
| Backwater valve install | Usually |
| Water service line replacement | Usually |

## Jobs that usually do not need a permit

- Clearing a clogged drain or sewer line
- Replacing a faucet cartridge or washer
- Fixing a running toilet
- Replacing a toilet in the same location, in many cases
- Small leak repairs on existing pipe

Rules can change, so check with the city before starting any project you are unsure about.

## Where permits come from

For homes inside the city, the City of Burien Permit Center handles building and plumbing permits. It is located at Burien City Hall, 400 SW 152nd Street. Side sewer work also involves your sewer district, which usually inspects the connection. Learn which district serves you in [Burien sewer districts](/blog/burien-sewer-districts/). Homes in unincorporated King County, such as parts of White Center, go through King County and Public Health Seattle and King County instead.

## Who pulls the permit?

A licensed plumbing contractor pulls the permit for the work they perform. Washington allows homeowners to do some work on their own single family home with a permit, but gas lines, sewer connections and water heaters carry real safety risks. Read [how to verify a plumber's license in Washington](/blog/verify-plumber-license-washington/) before hiring.

## What inspections check

- **Water heaters.** Seismic straps, relief valve and discharge pipe, venting, gas connection and expansion tank.
- **Repipes.** Pressure test, pipe support and proper materials.
- **Gas piping.** Pressure test and correct sizing.
- **Side sewers.** Pipe material, slope, bedding and the connection at the main.

## Why skipping a permit is risky

- Insurance may deny claims tied to unpermitted work.
- Buyers may ask for credits or for work to be redone.
- Unsafe installs can cause gas leaks, floods or carbon monoxide problems.
- The city can require corrections later.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Burien?
Yes. Water heater replacement requires a permit and inspection. A licensed plumber includes this in the job.

### Can a homeowner pull a plumbing permit?
In many cases, a homeowner can pull a permit for work on their own single family home. Check with the City of Burien Permit Center first.

### How long does a plumbing permit take?
Simple permits, like water heaters, are often issued quickly. Larger projects can take longer for review.

### What happens if work was done without a permit?
You may need to apply for a permit after the fact and have the work inspected, which can require opening walls.
