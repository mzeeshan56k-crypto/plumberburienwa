Polybutylene pipe is a gray, blue or black flexible plastic water line installed in many homes from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s. It can break down from the inside when exposed to chlorine in water, leading to sudden leaks with little warning. If your home has polybutylene, plan to replace it. Many insurers and buyers treat it as a known risk.

Most Burien homes are older than the polybutylene era and started with galvanized pipe. But some homes built or remodeled from the late 1970s into the 1990s, including homes in SeaTac, Normandy Park and parts of Burien, may have polybutylene supply lines. It was also used for some yard service lines. Knowing whether you have it is the first step.

## How to identify polybutylene

| Feature | Polybutylene |
| --- | --- |
| Color | Gray most often, also blue or black |
| Texture | Smooth, flexible plastic |
| Markings | Often stamped "PB2110" or similar |
| Fittings | Plastic or copper crimp rings |
| Size | Usually 1/2 inch to 1 inch |
| Where to look | Near the water heater, under sinks, in the crawl space, where the line enters the house |

Do not confuse it with PEX, which is also plastic. PEX is usually red, blue or white and is stamped "PEX". Gray polybutylene is the key clue.

## Why polybutylene fails

- Chlorine and other disinfectants in tap water break down the plastic from the inside.
- The pipe becomes brittle and flakes.
- Early plastic fittings crack.
- Failures can be sudden, with no visible warning.

## Problems it can cause

- Sudden leaks and floods.
- Higher insurance premiums or coverage limits.
- Lower appeal to buyers.
- Inspection reports that call for replacement.

## Should you replace it before it leaks?

Yes, in most cases. Because failures can be sudden, waiting can mean water damage that costs more than the repipe. If you are selling, replacing it removes a common buyer objection. If you are buying, ask for a credit or a price reduction. Read [buying an older home](/blog/older-home-plumbing-inspection/) for other plumbing checks before closing.

## What to do until you replace it

- Keep water pressure in the normal range of 40 to 80 psi.
- Check exposed pipe and fittings for cracks or flaking.
- Know where your main shutoff is. See [main water shutoff valve](/blog/main-water-shutoff-valve/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is polybutylene pipe still legal?
It is no longer used in new construction, but existing pipe is not illegal. Many inspectors and insurers recommend replacing it.

### How long does polybutylene pipe last?
It varies. Some lines leak within 10 to 15 years, while others last longer. The risk of failure grows with age.

### Is gray plastic pipe always polybutylene?
Usually, if it is flexible and in a home from the late 1970s to mid 1990s. Check the stamp on the pipe to confirm.

### Does polybutylene affect home insurance?
Some insurers charge more, limit coverage or decline homes with polybutylene. Ask your agent.
