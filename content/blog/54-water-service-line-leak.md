A water service line leak is a leak in the buried pipe between your water meter and your house. Signs include a water meter that keeps moving with the house shutoff closed, soggy spots in the yard, lower water pressure and a rising water bill. In most cases, the homeowner is responsible for the line from the meter to the house, while the water district maintains the meter and the line on the street side.

Many older Burien homes still have their original service lines. These may be galvanized steel, which rusts through, or early plastic lines like polybutylene, which can crack. Tree roots, soil movement on slopes and freezing can also damage them. Because the line is buried, leaks can run for months in our wet soil without obvious puddles.

## Signs of a service line leak

- The meter leak indicator moves with the house main valve closed.
- Soggy, sunken or greener patches in the yard between the meter and the house.
- Low water pressure throughout the house.
- Water bill rising with no change in use.
- Water bubbling up near the meter box.
- Dirty or cloudy water after the leak starts.

## How to confirm it

1. Turn off all water use inside and outside.
2. Close the main shutoff valve at the house.
3. Check the meter's leak indicator. If it still moves, the leak is between the meter and the house shutoff.

## Who is responsible?

| Section | Usually responsible |
| --- | --- |
| Water main in the street | Water district |
| Meter and meter box | Water district |
| Service line from meter to house | Homeowner |
| Pipes inside the house | Homeowner |

Burien is served by Highline Water District and King County Water Districts 20, 49 and 125. If the leak is right at the meter, call your district first. They may repair their side at no cost to you.

## Repair options and costs

| Job | Typical Burien area cost |
| --- | --- |
| Leak detection | $200 to $600 |
| Yard water service line repair | $800 to $3,000 |
| Water service line replacement | $2,500 to $6,000 |

A spot repair fixes one leak. If the line is old galvanized or polybutylene, replacement is often better. Many plumbers replace the line with PEX or polyethylene pipe. Trenchless methods can pull a new line through with minimal digging. If your home also has [polybutylene pipe](/blog/polybutylene-pipes/) inside, consider replacing both together.

## Why it matters

A service line leak wastes water you pay for, can wash out soil near your foundation and can lower pressure throughout the house. See [why is my water bill so high](/blog/high-water-bill-causes/) and [low water pressure causes](/blog/low-water-pressure-causes/) for related symptoms.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if my water line is leaking underground?
The best sign is a meter that keeps moving with all water off and the house valve closed. Soggy patches in the yard are another clue.

### Does homeowners insurance cover a service line leak?
Many policies do not cover the line itself. Some insurers and utilities offer service line coverage as an add on.

### How long does a service line replacement take?
Most take one day, depending on length, depth and access.

### Will my water district fix the leak?
Only if it is on their side of the meter. The line from the meter to the house is usually the homeowner's to repair.
