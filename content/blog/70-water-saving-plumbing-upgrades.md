The best water saving plumbing upgrades for Burien homes are fixing leaks, installing high efficiency toilets, adding low flow faucet aerators and shower heads, using leak sensors or automatic shutoff valves, and choosing an efficient water heater. Most homes can cut water use noticeably with a few low cost changes. Hot water savings also lower energy bills.

Water in the Burien area is billed by your water district, and sewer charges are often tied to water use as well. That means every gallon saved can lower two bills. Older homes, built when toilets used 3.5 gallons or more per flush, have the most to gain. Upgrades also reduce wear on aging pipes and fixtures.

## Start with leaks

Fixing leaks is the cheapest upgrade of all. Test toilets with the dye test in [how to check a toilet for leaks](/blog/check-toilet-for-leaks/), fix dripping faucets with our [leaky faucet repair](/blog/leaky-faucet-repair/) guide, and watch the water meter for hidden leaks.

## Efficient toilets

Older toilets can use 3.5 to 5 gallons per flush. Modern WaterSense toilets use 1.28 gallons or less and still flush well. Replacing one old toilet in a busy household can save thousands of gallons a year.

## Faucets and shower heads

Aerators and efficient shower heads are among the easiest upgrades. Look for the WaterSense label, which means the product meets EPA standards for efficiency and performance. Less hot water used in the shower also means less energy used by the water heater.

## Hot water efficiency

- Set the water heater to about 120°F.
- Insulate exposed hot water pipes in the crawl space.
- Consider a heat pump water heater for electric homes. Puget Sound Energy has offered rebates on qualifying models. See [heat pump water heaters](/blog/heat-pump-water-heater/).
- Fix leaks on the hot side quickly.

## Smart leak protection

Leak sensors near the water heater, washer and sinks send alerts to your phone. An automatic shutoff valve on the main line closes when it detects a leak. These are especially useful for homes that are often empty, as described in our [vacation plumbing checklist](/blog/vacation-plumbing-checklist/).

## Outdoor water use

- Fix leaking hose bibs and irrigation lines.
- Water early in the morning.
- Use a timer on irrigation systems.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest way to save water at home?
Fix leaks and running toilets first. Then add efficient aerators and shower heads.

### Do low flow toilets work well?
Modern WaterSense toilets are designed to flush well while using 1.28 gallons or less. Older early low flow models had problems that newer designs solved.

### Will saving water lower my sewer bill too?
Often yes, because sewer charges in many areas are based on water use. Check your sewer district's billing method.

### Are there rebates for water saving upgrades?
Rebates change over time. Check with Puget Sound Energy for water heater rebates and your water district for any conservation programs.
