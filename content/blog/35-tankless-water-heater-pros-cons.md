The main tankless water heater pros are endless hot water, lower standby energy use, a longer lifespan and a small wall mounted unit that frees up floor space. The main cons are a higher installed cost, often $3,500 to $7,000 in Burien, possible gas line and venting upgrades, a short delay before hot water arrives, and yearly descaling. For many families, the long life and steady supply make up for the higher upfront price.

A tank heater keeps 40 to 50 gallons hot all day. A tankless unit heats water only when you open a tap. Water passes through a heat exchanger, and a powerful burner or heating element warms it on demand. In Burien, most tankless units are gas fired, because whole house electric tankless models need very large electrical service.

## Pros of tankless water heaters

- **Endless hot water.** Back to back showers do not run the tank dry.
- **Lower energy use.** No standby heat loss from a storage tank.
- **Longer lifespan.** Often 15 to 20 years with maintenance, compared to 8 to 12 years for a tank.
- **Space savings.** Mounts on a wall, freeing up a closet or garage corner.
- **Less flood risk.** No tank that can rupture.

## Cons of tankless water heaters

- **Higher upfront cost.** Installation costs more than a tank.
- **Gas line upgrades.** Many units need a larger gas line than a tank heater.
- **New venting.** Tankless units need specific vent pipe, which may mean new wall or roof penetrations.
- **Flow limits.** Running many fixtures at once can exceed the unit's capacity.
- **Short hot water delay.** A few seconds of cold water before hot arrives.
- **Annual maintenance.** Descaling and filter cleaning keep it running well.

## Sizing a tankless unit

Tankless heaters are sized by flow rate in gallons per minute and by the temperature rise needed. In winter, incoming water in the Puget Sound area is cold, so the heater must raise the temperature more. A family that runs two showers and a dishwasher at once needs a larger unit. ## Is tankless right for your Burien home?

Tankless makes sense if you plan to stay in the home for years, your family uses a lot of hot water, or you need the space. A tank may be better if your budget is tight or your gas line is small. If you have an electric home, compare a [heat pump water heater](/blog/heat-pump-water-heater/) too.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do tankless water heaters really save money?
They use less energy than a tank, which lowers monthly bills. The higher install cost means savings take years to repay, but the longer lifespan helps.

### Can a tankless water heater run out of hot water?
It will not run out, but it can be overwhelmed if too many fixtures run at once. Proper sizing prevents that.

### Does a tankless water heater work during a power outage?
Most gas tankless units need electricity for controls and fans, so they stop during an outage.

### How often does a tankless water heater need maintenance?
Once a year. A plumber flushes the heat exchanger to remove scale and cleans the inlet filter.
