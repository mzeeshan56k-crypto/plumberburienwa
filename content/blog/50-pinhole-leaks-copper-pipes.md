Pinhole leaks in copper pipes are tiny holes caused by corrosion that eats through the pipe wall from the inside. Common causes include water chemistry, high water velocity, high pressure, poor original soldering and age. A single pinhole can be repaired by replacing that section. Two or more pinholes within a year or two usually mean the copper is failing and a repipe is the better long term fix.

Copper became common in Burien homes from the 1960s onward, often replacing galvanized steel during remodels. Many of those lines are now 40 to 60 years old. Pinholes often show up first as a small drip, a spray inside a wall or a green crust on the pipe. In crawl space homes, they can drip for weeks before anyone notices.

## Signs of a pinhole leak

- A small spray or drip from a copper pipe.
- Green or blue green crust on the outside of the pipe.
- Water stains on ceilings or walls.
- Damp insulation in the crawl space.
- A water bill that creeps up.
- The sound of hissing behind a wall.

## Why copper pipes develop pinholes

| Cause | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Water chemistry | Certain pH levels and dissolved oxygen attack the copper |
| High velocity | Fast flowing water wears thin spots, especially at elbows |
| High pressure | Above 80 psi stresses the pipe |
| Debris or flux left inside | Spots of corrosion start where debris settles |
| Thin wall pipe | Type M copper has thinner walls than Type L |
| Electrical grounding issues | Stray current can speed up corrosion |
| Age | Pipe wall slowly thins over decades |

## Temporary fixes

- **Repair clamp.** A rubber gasket and metal clamp seal the hole for a short time.
- **Push to connect fitting.** A plumber or skilled homeowner can cut out the pinhole and install a short new section.
- **Epoxy putty.** Only for emergency use until a proper repair.

## When to repipe

If you have had a second or third pinhole leak in a year, the pipe wall is likely thin throughout. Patching one leak at a time leads to repeated repairs and water damage. ## Check your water pressure

High pressure speeds up pinhole leaks. A gauge on a hose bib should read 40 to 80 psi. If it is higher, a failing pressure reducing valve may be the cause. See [pressure reducing valves](/blog/pressure-reducing-valve/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Are pinhole leaks covered by insurance?
Water damage from a sudden leak is often covered. The cost to replace the corroded pipe usually is not.

### Can pinhole leaks be prevented?
Keeping water pressure in the normal range and using proper installation helps. Once pipe has thinned with age, more leaks are likely.

### Is one pinhole leak a sign of more to come?
Sometimes it is a single defect. If a second leak appears within a year or two, the pipe is likely failing throughout.

### Do pipe coatings or epoxy lining fix pinholes?
Interior epoxy lining is an option in some cases, but most homeowners choose repiping for a lasting fix.
