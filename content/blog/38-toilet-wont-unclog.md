If a toilet won't unclog with a plunger, first make sure you are using a flange plunger and getting a good seal. If that fails, use a toilet auger, also called a closet auger, to reach the clog in the trap. If water rises in the tub or other drains when you flush, stop. The clog is in the main line, and plunging will only push sewage into the lowest drain.

Toilets clog more often in older homes with older low flow toilets and narrow drain lines. Common causes include too much toilet paper, wipes, paper towels and small objects. In Burien homes with clay side sewers, roots in the main line can also make toilets slow or clog often.

## First: stop an overflow

1. Remove the tank lid.
2. Push the flapper down to stop water from entering the bowl.
3. Turn off the water supply valve behind the toilet, clockwise.
4. Do not flush again until the clog is clear.

## Step 1: Use the right plunger

A flange plunger has a soft extension on the bottom that fits the toilet drain. A flat cup plunger is for sinks.

- Make sure there is enough water in the bowl to cover the plunger.
- Push down gently first to push out air, then plunge firmly 15 to 20 times.
- Keep the seal and pull up sharply.

## Step 2: Use a toilet auger

A toilet auger has a bent tube with a rubber sleeve to protect the porcelain.

1. Place the curved end in the drain opening.
2. Crank the handle while pushing the cable in.
3. When you feel resistance, keep turning to break up or hook the clog.
4. Pull the cable back and flush to test.

## Things not to do

- Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a toilet. It can crack the porcelain and splash back. See [chemical drain cleaners](/blog/chemical-drain-cleaners/).
- Do not keep flushing to test.
- Do not use a wire hanger, which can scratch the bowl.

## Signs the clog is in the main line

| Sign | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Tub or shower fills when you flush | Main line clog |
| Toilet gurgles when the sink drains | Shared line or vent problem |
| Several toilets clog at once | Main line clog |
| Basement floor drain backs up | Main line or side sewer clog |

If you see these signs, read [gurgling drains](/blog/gurgling-drains/) and [basement floor drain backing up](/blog/basement-floor-drain-backup/). A main line clog needs a plumber with a drain machine.

## Why toilets clog again and again

- An older low flow toilet with a weak flush.
- A partial clog deep in the trap, like a toy or a hair tie.
- Wipes, even ones labeled flushable.
- Roots or a sag in the side sewer.
- A blocked vent.

A plumber can pull the toilet and clear the trap from below, or camera the line to find a deeper cause.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a clogged toilet eventually unclog itself?
A clog of toilet paper can soften and clear on its own over hours. Wipes, objects and heavy clogs will not.

### Is it safe to use dish soap and hot water on a toilet clog?
A squirt of dish soap and hot, not boiling, water can help soften a paper clog. Boiling water can crack porcelain.

### Why does my toilet clog every time I flush?
Often an object is caught in the trap, the toilet has a weak flush, or there is a problem in the main line. A plumber can find the cause.

### When should I call a plumber for a clogged toilet?
Call if plunging and an auger do not work, if other drains back up, or if the toilet clogs often.
