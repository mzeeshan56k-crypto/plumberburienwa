Before buying an older home in Burien, check five plumbing items: the side sewer with a camera, the type and age of the water pipes, the water heater's age and condition, the water pressure, and the crawl space for leaks and moisture. A standard home inspection only looks at visible plumbing, so order a sewer scope separately. These checks can reveal repairs worth thousands before you commit.

Much of Burien's housing was built in the 1940s through the 1960s. Many of those homes have been updated over the years, but plumbing upgrades are often partial. A remodeled kitchen can sit on top of original galvanized supply lines and a clay side sewer. Knowing what you are buying lets you negotiate, plan repairs and avoid surprises after move in.

## The five checks that matter most

| Check | Why it matters | Possible cost if it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sewer scope | Finds roots, cracks, bellies in the side sewer | $2,500 to $25,000 |
| Pipe type | Galvanized and polybutylene need replacing | $8,000 to $20,000 for a repipe |
| Water heater age | Tanks last 8 to 12 years | $1,900 to $3,800 |
| Water pressure | Low pressure points to old pipe, high pressure strains fixtures | $350 to $800 for a pressure valve |
| Crawl space | Leaks, moisture, sagging drains | $200 to $1,500 for typical repairs |

## 1. Get a sewer scope

A camera inspection of the side sewer is the single most valuable plumbing check for an older home. It shows the pipe material and any roots, cracks, offsets or sags. Read [sewer scope inspection in Burien](/blog/sewer-scope-inspection/) for what to expect.

## 2. Identify the water pipes

Look where the main line enters the house, near the water heater and in the crawl space.

- **Galvanized steel.** Gray metal with threaded joints. Likely near the end of its life. See [galvanized pipes in older homes](/blog/galvanized-pipes-older-homes/).
- **Copper.** Reddish metal. Check for green crust and patched sections.
- **Polybutylene.** Gray plastic, common in late 1970s to mid 1990s work. Plan to replace it.
- **PEX.** Red, blue or white plastic. Usually a recent upgrade.

## 3. Check the water heater

Find the age from the serial number on the label. Look for rust, water at the base, seismic straps and a proper vent. If it is over 10 years old, budget for replacement.

## 4. Test water pressure and flow

Turn on the kitchen faucet and a shower at the same time. Flush a toilet. Weak flow suggests old pipe. A pressure gauge on a hose bib should read 40 to 80 psi.

## 5. Look in the crawl space

Check for standing water, wet insulation, drips, rusty pipe and sagging drain lines. Our guide to [crawl space plumbing problems](/blog/crawl-space-plumbing-problems/) lists what to look for.

## Other things to ask the seller

- Has the side sewer ever been repaired or replaced?
- Have there been any backups or leaks?
- Were permits pulled for plumbing work?
- Is there a sewer cleanout?
- When was the water heater installed?

## Use findings to negotiate

If inspections reveal major issues, you can ask the seller to repair them, offer a credit or adjust the price. Get written quotes from a licensed plumber to support your request.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a home inspection include the sewer line?
Usually not. Most home inspectors only test visible plumbing. Order a sewer scope separately.

### What is the most expensive plumbing problem in older homes?
Side sewer replacement and whole house repiping are usually the largest costs.

### Should I walk away from a home with galvanized pipes?
Not necessarily. Galvanized pipe is common in older Burien homes. Factor the cost of a repipe into your offer.

### How long does a sewer scope take during escrow?
Most take under an hour, and the results are usually available the same day.
