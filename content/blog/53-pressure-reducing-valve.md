A pressure reducing valve, or PRV, lowers the water pressure coming from the street to a safe level for your home, usually around 50 to 70 psi. Signs your PRV is failing include water pressure over 80 psi, banging pipes, dripping faucets, running toilets, a leaking water heater relief valve, or a sudden drop in pressure. Most PRVs last 10 to 15 years and then need replacing.

Water districts must keep enough pressure in their mains to serve hills, fire hydrants and tall buildings. In some parts of Burien and nearby areas, especially lower elevations near Puget Sound, street pressure can be well above what home plumbing needs. A PRV near where the main line enters the house brings it down. When the valve wears out, your home feels the full street pressure.

## Signs of a failing PRV

| Sign | What is happening |
| --- | --- |
| Pressure gauge reads over 80 psi | PRV no longer reducing |
| Banging pipes | High pressure causing water hammer |
| Faucets drip, toilets run | Valves cannot hold back high pressure |
| Water heater relief valve drips | Pressure building in the tank |
| Pressure swings up and down | Worn diaphragm or spring |
| Sudden low pressure | PRV stuck partly closed |
| Humming or vibration in pipes | Valve chattering |

## How to test your water pressure

1. Buy a pressure gauge that threads onto a hose bib.
2. Turn off all water in the house.
3. Attach the gauge to the hose bib nearest the main line.
4. Open the hose bib fully and read the gauge.
5. Check again at night, when street pressure is often highest.

A reading between 40 and 80 psi is normal. 50 to 60 psi is ideal for most homes.

## Where is the PRV?

It is usually on the main line just after the main shutoff valve. Look in the crawl space, basement, garage or near the water heater. It looks like a bell shaped brass valve with an adjustment screw on top. Some are in the meter box. If you are not sure where your main is, see [main water shutoff valve](/blog/main-water-shutoff-valve/).

## Why you need an expansion tank with a PRV

A PRV acts like a one way valve. When the water heater heats water, the water expands and has nowhere to go. Pressure rises, and the relief valve drips. An expansion tank on the cold line near the water heater absorbs that expansion. Code often requires one when a PRV is present.

## Why it matters

High pressure wears out every fixture in the house. It shortens the life of faucets, toilet fill valves, washing machine hoses and water heaters. It can also cause [banging pipes](/blog/water-hammer-banging-pipes/) and speed up [pinhole leaks in copper pipes](/blog/pinhole-leaks-copper-pipes/). ## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a pressure reducing valve last?
Most last 10 to 15 years. Sediment and wear on the internal diaphragm cause them to fail over time.

### Can I adjust my PRV myself?
You can turn the adjustment screw a small amount with a gauge attached. If the valve does not respond or keeps drifting, replace it.

### Does every home have a PRV?
No. Homes in areas with normal street pressure may not need one. A gauge test tells you whether yours does.

### What happens if water pressure is too high?
Pipes, fittings, valves and appliances wear out faster, and leaks become more likely.
