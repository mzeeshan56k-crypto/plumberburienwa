If you have no hot water, start with the simple checks. On an electric water heater, check the breaker and the reset button. On a gas heater, check that the pilot light is lit and the gas valve is on. Then look for leaks, a tripped high limit switch or a thermostat set too low. If the tank leaks or smells of gas, stop and call a professional.

Most no hot water calls in Burien come in fall and winter, when incoming water is colder and heaters work harder. A worn part that held on all summer finally fails. Many problems take minutes to fix. Others mean the heater has reached the end of its life.

## First: is it all fixtures or just one?

| What you notice | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| No hot water anywhere | Water heater, power or gas supply |
| No hot water at one faucet | Faucet cartridge or mixing valve |
| Hot water runs out fast | Sediment, a failed element or an undersized tank |
| Lukewarm water only | One failed element or a thermostat issue |

## 8 things to check

1. **The breaker (electric).** Look for a tripped double pole breaker labeled water heater. Reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call.
2. **The reset button (electric).** Turn off the power, remove the upper access panel and press the red high limit reset button. If it trips again soon, a thermostat or element has failed.
3. **The pilot light (gas).** Look through the window at the bottom of the tank. If the pilot is out, follow the lighting steps on the label.
4. **The gas supply.** Make sure the gas valve on the line is open and other gas appliances work. If you smell gas, leave the house and call Puget Sound Energy or 911.
5. **The thermostat setting.** Someone may have turned it down. Set it to about 120°F.
6. **The vent (gas).** A blocked flue can shut down some newer gas heaters. Look for debris or a disconnected vent pipe.
7. **Leaks.** Check for water around the base. A leaking tank needs replacing.
8. **Tankless error codes.** Read the display. Many codes point to gas supply, venting or scale buildup.

## Hot water runs out too fast

Sediment at the bottom of the tank takes up space and insulates the water from the burner. Flushing the tank can help. Our guide on [how to flush a water heater](/blog/flush-water-heater/) shows the steps. If your household has grown, the tank may simply be too small.

## Safety first

- Never ignore a gas smell.
- Do not open the relief valve if it is very hot or the tank is overheating.
- Turn off power before removing access panels.
- Do not keep restarting a heater that keeps shutting off.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why did my water heater suddenly stop working?
The most common causes are a tripped breaker, a failed heating element, a pilot light that went out or a failed thermocouple. A tank leak can also trip the system.

### How do I reset my electric water heater?
Turn off the power, remove the upper panel and insulation, and press the red reset button. Replace the panel and turn the power back on. If it trips again, call a plumber.

### Why do I have hot water in one bathroom but not another?
That points to the faucet or shower valve, not the water heater. A worn cartridge or mixing valve is the usual cause.

### How long should it take to get hot water back after a repair?
A typical 40 to 50 gallon tank needs about 30 to 60 minutes to heat fully after a repair or refill.
