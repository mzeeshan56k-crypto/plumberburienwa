For landlords in Burien, good plumbing management means three things: respond quickly to repair requests, do preventive maintenance on a schedule, and give tenants clear guidance on what not to flush or pour down drains. Washington law requires landlords to keep plumbing in good working order and provide hot and cold water, with short repair timelines for urgent problems like loss of water.

Burien has many rental homes, duplexes and small apartment buildings, along with rentals in White Center, Boulevard Park and SeaTac. Older rental properties often have galvanized pipe, clay side sewers and aging water heaters. Tenants may not report a slow leak or a running toilet, so small problems can grow into large ones between turnovers.

## Your responsibilities under Washington law

Washington's Residential Landlord Tenant Act requires landlords to keep plumbing fixtures and water heaters in working order. When a tenant gives written notice of a problem, the law sets deadlines to start repairs. The deadline is short, as little as 24 hours, for urgent problems like no hot or cold water. Check the current law and talk to an attorney for specific situations.

## Preventive maintenance schedule

| Task | How often |
| --- | --- |
| Check under sinks and around toilets for leaks | At every inspection and turnover |
| Test toilets with the dye test | Twice a year |
| Flush water heaters | Yearly |
| Replace washer and toilet supply hoses | Every 5 years |
| Camera the side sewer in older buildings | Every few years or after backups |
| Winterize outdoor faucets | Every fall |
| Test main shutoff valves | Yearly |

## Turnover checklist

1. Run every faucet and shower and check drainage.
2. Flush every toilet and check for running or rocking.
3. Look under sinks for drips and damage.
4. Check the water heater age, straps and relief valve.
5. Check the crawl space or basement for leaks.
6. Replace worn aerators and shower heads.
7. Confirm tenants know where the main shutoff is.

## Give tenants clear guidance

A short handout reduces repair calls:

- Do not flush wipes, paper towels or hygiene products.
- Do not pour grease down the kitchen sink. See [grease clog prevention](/blog/grease-clog-prevention/).
- Run cold water with the garbage disposal and keep bones, peels and coffee grounds out of it.
- Report leaks and running toilets right away.
- Keep the heat on in winter. See [how to prevent frozen pipes](/blog/prevent-frozen-pipes/).
- Know where the water shutoff is.

## Control water costs

If you pay for water, a running toilet or hidden leak can be costly. Watch bills for spikes and act on them. Read [why is my water bill so high](/blog/high-water-bill-causes/) for common causes.

## Plan for aging systems

Replace water heaters before they fail, especially in units where a leak could damage floors below. Budget for repiping buildings with failing galvanized pipe. Planned work costs less than emergency repairs and tenant displacement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who pays for a clogged drain in a rental?
It depends on the cause and the lease. Landlords generally handle repairs from normal wear. Clogs caused by tenant misuse may be charged back if the lease allows.

### How fast must a landlord fix a water heater in Washington?
Loss of hot water is treated as urgent. Repairs must begin quickly after written notice, often within 24 hours.

### Should landlords replace water heaters before they fail?
For units over 10 years old, yes. A planned replacement avoids flood damage and emergency costs.

### Can tenants make plumbing repairs themselves?
Only if the lease allows it and the landlord agrees. Most landlords prefer to use a licensed plumber.
