A plumbing emergency is any problem that is causing damage right now or poses a health or safety risk. Water spreading across floors, water coming through a ceiling, a sewage backup, no water in the house, a gas smell or a leaking water heater tank are emergencies. A dripping faucet, a running toilet or one slow drain can usually wait for a scheduled visit.

Knowing the difference saves money and stress. After hours calls cost more, so it makes sense to know which problems truly cannot wait. At the same time, waiting on a real emergency can turn a $500 repair into a $5,000 cleanup. This checklist helps you decide in a minute.

## Emergency or can it wait?

| Call now | Can usually wait |
| --- | --- |
| Burst or spraying pipe | Dripping faucet |
| Water coming through a ceiling | Running toilet |
| Sewage backing up into a tub or floor drain | One slow drain |
| No water in the whole house | Low pressure at one fixture |
| Leaking water heater tank | Water heater making noise |
| Gas smell | Small leak you can catch in a bucket |
| Toilet overflowing and will not stop | Clogged toilet in a home with another bathroom |
| Frozen pipe that has split | Frozen pipe with no leak yet |

## What to do first in any emergency

1. **Stop the water.** Close the fixture shutoff or the main water valve. Our guide to the [main water shutoff valve](/blog/main-water-shutoff-valve/) shows where to look.
2. **Make it safe.** Turn off electricity to wet areas if you can do so safely.
3. **Stop using water.** For sewage backups, stop all sinks, toilets, showers and appliances.
4. **Protect belongings.** Move items and start drying.
5. **Call for help.** Describe what you see so the plumber brings the right tools.

## If you smell gas

Leave the house right away. Do not use switches, phones or anything that could spark inside. From outside, call Puget Sound Energy's emergency line or 911. A plumber can repair gas piping after the utility has made it safe.

## Sewage backup

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and pets away, do not use any drains, and call a plumber. A backup in the lowest drain usually means a main line or side sewer clog. Read [basement floor drain backing up](/blog/basement-floor-drain-backup/) for what causes it.

## Water through the ceiling

Shut off the main water. If the ceiling is sagging with water, poke a small hole in the lowest point with a screwdriver and catch the water in a bucket. This prevents a larger collapse. Turn off lights and power in that area. See [ceiling water stains](/blog/ceiling-water-stain-leak/) for what usually causes them.

## Problems that can wait until morning

If the water is off at the fixture and nothing is getting worse, you can usually wait. A running toilet can be shut off at its valve. A dripping faucet can be closed under the sink. Booking a daytime visit avoids after hours fees. Our article on [emergency plumber costs](/blog/emergency-plumber-cost/) explains what changes at night and on weekends.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a clogged toilet an emergency?
Only if it is the only toilet in the house or it is overflowing and will not stop. Otherwise, shut off the supply valve and schedule a daytime visit.

### Is a leaking water heater an emergency?
A leak from the tank itself is, because it can fail suddenly. Turn off the water and power or gas to the heater and call a plumber.

### Should I call a plumber or my insurance first?
Call the plumber first to stop the damage. Then call your insurer and keep photos and receipts.

### Is no hot water an emergency?
Usually not, unless the heater is leaking or you smell gas. You can often wait for a daytime appointment.
