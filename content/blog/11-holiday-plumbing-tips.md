These holiday plumbing tips help you avoid the most common problems when the house is full: grease clogs in the kitchen sink, a jammed garbage disposal, overworked toilets and a water heater that runs out of hot water. Keep fats out of the drain, feed the disposal slowly, space out showers and keep a plunger in every bathroom.

The day after Thanksgiving is one of the busiest days of the year for plumbers. Big meals mean grease, potato peels and bones in the sink. Extra guests mean more flushes and back to back showers. In older Burien homes with narrow galvanized or cast iron drain lines, that extra load is often what turns a slow drain into a full backup.

## Kitchen: keep fats and scraps out of the drain

- Pour turkey fat and pan drippings into a can or jar. Let it cool and throw it away.
- Wipe greasy pans and roasting trays with paper towels before washing.
- Scrape plates into the trash or compost, not the sink.
- Use a sink strainer during cleanup.
- Run the dishwasher only when full, and not at the same time as the washing machine.

Our article on [kitchen grease clog prevention](/blog/grease-clog-prevention/) explains why grease is the number one cause of kitchen backups.

## Garbage disposal: what it cannot handle

| Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Potato peels | Turn into a thick paste that clogs the line |
| Turkey bones | Can jam the blades |
| Celery and onion skins | Stringy fibers wrap around parts |
| Coffee grounds | Settle in the pipe like sand |
| Rice and pasta | Swell with water |
| Grease and gravy | Harden in the pipe |

Run cold water before, during and for 15 seconds after using the disposal. Feed food in small amounts. See [garbage disposal dos and don'ts](/blog/garbage-disposal-dos-and-donts/) for the full list.

## Bathrooms: more guests, more flushes

- Keep a plunger in each bathroom so guests can deal with a small clog.
- Put a small trash can with a lid next to every toilet.
- Tell guests not to flush wipes, even ones labeled flushable.
- Wait a few seconds between flushes if a toilet is slow to refill.

If a toilet does clog, our guide to [a toilet that won't unclog](/blog/toilet-wont-unclog/) explains what to try and when to stop.

## Showers and hot water

- Space showers 10 to 15 minutes apart so the water heater can recover.
- Run the dishwasher at night when no one is showering.
- If you have a full house for several days, consider raising the water heater temperature slightly, but keep it at or below 120°F to prevent scalding.

## Before guests arrive

1. Fix any slow drains now. A slow drain under normal use will back up under holiday use.
2. Test that every toilet flushes fully and stops running.
3. Check under sinks for drips.
4. Know where the main water shutoff is. Read [how to find your main water shutoff](/blog/main-water-shutoff-valve/) if you are not sure.

## Warning signs of a bigger problem

- Water backs up into the tub when you run the dishwasher.
- Toilets gurgle when the kitchen sink drains.
- More than one drain is slow at once.
- A sewer smell in the house.

These point to a main line clog, not a single fixture. Stop using water and call for help. If you have a backup during the holidays, our [Burien emergency plumbers](/) answer 24/7 at (833) 710-2588.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do plumbing problems spike after Thanksgiving?
Large amounts of grease, starchy scraps and extra guests overload kitchen drains and toilets on the same day. Lines that were already narrowed by buildup finally clog.

### Can I put turkey grease down the drain with hot water?
No. Hot water only moves grease farther down the pipe, where it cools and hardens. Let grease cool in a container and throw it away.

### What should I do if my sink backs up during a party?
Stop running water in the house, especially the dishwasher and washing machine. Try a plunger on the sink. If other drains are affected, call a plumber.

### Do plumbers charge more on holidays?
Many do. Holiday and after hours visits often add $100 to $300. Ask for the price range on the phone before the plumber comes out.
