The most common signs of a hidden water leak are a water bill that jumps with no change in use, a water meter that moves when every fixture is off, damp or stained spots on walls and ceilings, a musty smell, and the sound of running water when nothing is on. Warm spots on the floor can point to a hot water slab leak.

Hidden leaks are common in older Burien homes. Galvanized pipe rusts through, copper develops pinholes and supply lines in crawl spaces leak where no one looks. Because many local homes sit over crawl spaces, a leak can run for months, soaking insulation and wood before anyone notices. Catching it early keeps the repair small.

## 9 warning signs

1. **Your water bill jumped.** Compare the same months from last year. A steady rise without new use is a red flag.
2. **The water meter moves when everything is off.** This is the clearest test. See the steps below.
3. **Sound of running water.** A hiss or trickle in a wall when no one is using water.
4. **Damp or stained drywall.** Yellow or brown rings on ceilings or walls.
5. **Musty smell.** Especially in a closet, cabinet or near the crawl space access.
6. **Warped or soft flooring.** Hardwood that cups or vinyl that bubbles.
7. **Warm spots on the floor.** A hot water line leaking under a slab can warm the floor above it.
8. **Mold or mildew.** In cabinets, around baseboards or on the underside of the subfloor.
9. **Low water pressure.** A large leak can reduce pressure at fixtures.

## How to check your water meter for a leak

1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation system.
2. Make sure toilets have finished filling.
3. Find your meter, usually in a box near the street.
4. Look at the leak indicator, a small triangle or dial that spins with any flow.
5. Write down the reading, wait 30 to 60 minutes without using water, and check again.

If the indicator moves or the reading changes, water is leaking somewhere. Next, close the main shutoff at the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is in the yard line between the meter and the house. Our guide to a [leaking water service line](/blog/water-service-line-leak/) covers that case.

## Rule out the toilets first

Running toilets are the most common source of wasted water. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color shows up in the bowl within 15 minutes without flushing, the flapper leaks. See [how to check a toilet for leaks](/blog/check-toilet-for-leaks/).

## Where hidden leaks happen most

| Location | Common cause |
| --- | --- |
| Crawl space supply lines | Corroded galvanized pipe, frozen and split lines |
| Under sinks | Loose supply lines and trap connections |
| Behind showers and tubs | Failed valve or drain connection |
| Under a slab | Pinhole leak in a buried copper line |
| Yard service line | Old pipe between the meter and the house |

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I tell if I have a leak behind a wall?
Look for stains, bubbling paint, soft drywall or a musty smell. A moving water meter with everything off confirms a leak somewhere in the system.

### Can a hidden leak raise my water bill a lot?
Yes. Even a small steady leak can waste thousands of gallons a month. A running toilet alone can waste hundreds of gallons a day.

### Does homeowners insurance cover hidden leaks?
Many policies cover sudden water damage but not slow leaks or the pipe repair itself. Check your policy, and document the damage with photos.

### How long does leak detection take?
Most leak detection visits take one to three hours. Complex leaks under slabs or in long yard lines can take longer.
