Tree roots in a sewer line enter through cracks and joints in older pipe, drawn by water, warmth and nutrients. Once inside, fine roots grow into thick masses that catch toilet paper and waste. Signs include slow drains throughout the house, gurgling toilets and backups in the lowest drain. Roots can be cut out, but they grow back unless the damaged pipe is lined or replaced.

Burien is full of mature trees. Douglas firs, maples, birches and willows grow on many local lots, especially in Seahurst, Gregory Heights and around Lake Burien. Many homes here have clay side sewers with joints every few feet. Each joint is a possible entry point. A tiny gap is enough for a root hair to get in, and from there it keeps growing.

## Warning signs of roots in the sewer

- Several drains are slow at the same time.
- Toilets gurgle or bubble when other fixtures drain.
- Water backs up into a basement floor drain or the lowest tub.
- Clogs come back every few months.
- Sewer smell in the yard.
- Soft or soggy spots in the lawn along the sewer path.
- Faster growing, greener grass over the line.

## Trees most likely to cause problems

| Tree | Root behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Willow | Aggressive, seeks water |
| Maple (silver and bigleaf) | Large, spreading roots |
| Birch | Shallow, water seeking roots |
| Poplar and cottonwood | Fast growing, invasive |
| Douglas fir | Large trees, wide root zone |

Any tree can send roots into a damaged pipe. Distance matters more than species.

## How long does it last?

Cutting roots clears the line, but the cracks and joints are still there. Roots usually grow back within one to two years. Many homeowners schedule cleaning every 12 to 18 months as a maintenance plan. That is cheaper short term, but it does not fix the pipe.

## Permanent fixes

- **Spot repair.** Replace the single bad section where roots enter. $2,500 to $7,500.
- **Trenchless lining.** A new seamless pipe is formed inside the old one, sealing every joint. $6,000 to $18,000.
- **Full replacement.** Dig or burst in a new pipe. $8,000 to $25,000.

Our article on [trenchless sewer repair](/blog/trenchless-sewer-repair/) explains lining and pipe bursting in detail.

## Find out first with a camera

A camera inspection shows how many joints have roots and how bad the damage is. Light roots at one joint are a maintenance item. Roots at three or more joints usually mean lining or replacement. Read [what a sewer scope shows](/blog/sewer-scope-inspection/) before you decide.

## Can you prevent roots?

- Plant new trees well away from the sewer path.
- Choose smaller trees with less aggressive roots near the line.
- Keep up with scheduled cleanings if roots are known.
- Replace clay pipe with plastic, which has sealed joints.

Some root killer products can slow regrowth, but they will not remove a heavy root mass or fix the pipe.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can tree roots break a sewer pipe?
Roots usually enter through existing cracks and joints. As they grow, they can widen cracks and shift sections, making the damage worse.

### Do I have to remove the tree?
Usually not. Lining or replacing the damaged pipe stops roots without removing the tree. Removal is only needed if the tree sits directly on the line.

### Does homeowners insurance cover root damage to sewer lines?
Most standard policies do not. Some offer a separate service line endorsement. Check with your agent.

### How often should roots be cleared from a sewer line?
If roots are known, every 12 to 18 months keeps most lines flowing until you plan a permanent repair.
