What plumbing repairs do SeaTac homes need most?

SeaTac's most common plumbing problems are aging galvanized water lines in mid-century homes, root-clogged side sewers, worn-out water heaters, and shared drain stack clogs in apartments and condos. Wet winters also strain yard drains and sump pumps in low spots.

Mid-century ramblers

Many SeaTac houses date from the 1950s and 1960s. If the plumbing has not been updated, the supply lines may be galvanized steel. That pipe rusts inside, so pressure drops and leaks start at threaded joints. The side sewer is often clay pipe, which lets roots in at the joints.

Townhomes and newer builds

Newer homes near Angle Lake and along the light rail line mostly use PEX supply lines. Problems there tend to be fixture and valve failures, water heater issues, and clogs from construction debris in the first few years.

Apartments and condos

In multi-unit buildings, one clog in a shared stack can back up into several units. Grease from kitchens is the top cause. Water heaters in these buildings also work hard and wear out faster.

Airport-area remodels

Some homes near the airport have had noise insulation work, which often included new windows and doors. That work generally does not touch the plumbing, so the original pipes may still be in place even in homes that look updated.

Who pays for plumbing repairs in SeaTac rentals and condos?

Renters should report leaks and clogs to the landlord right away, in writing. Landlords can call us directly and approve the quote by phone. Washington law requires landlords to keep plumbing in good working order, and fast reports help limit damage to the unit and the building.

If you are a renter facing an active leak at night and cannot reach your landlord, shut off the water to the fixture or unit if you can. Then keep trying to reach the owner. We can talk you through shutoff steps over the phone even if the owner will be the one booking the repair. Landlords with several SeaTac units can set up a single contact for approvals and invoices.
