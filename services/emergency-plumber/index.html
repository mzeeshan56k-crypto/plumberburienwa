When should you call an emergency plumber right away?

You need an emergency plumber now if water is moving where it should not be, sewage is coming up, or you cannot shut the water off. Waiting even a few hours can mean wet drywall, ruined flooring, and mold. If you can stop the water and use one bathroom, it can often wait until morning.

  • Water spraying from a pipe, fitting, or supply line
  • A wet, sagging, or dripping ceiling
  • Sewage or dirty water rising in a tub, shower, or floor drain
  • A water heater leaking from the tank
  • A shutoff valve that will not close
  • No water in the whole house
  • Gas smell near a water heater. Leave the house and call your gas utility first.

Yes. Burien Plumbers answers calls 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Call (833) 710-2588 and tell us your address. We give you a real arrival window.

How do I shut off my water in an emergency?

Find the main valve where the water line enters your home, often in the crawl space, basement, or garage. Turn it clockwise until it stops. If it will not move, the meter box near the street has a second shutoff.

Will my homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe?

Many policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe but not slow leaks or the pipe itself. Take photos, keep your plumber invoice, and call your insurer. We can give you notes on the cause.

Do emergency plumbers charge more on weekends?

Yes. Nights, weekends, and holidays usually add $100 to $300 to the service fee. We tell you the exact fee on the phone before we book.

What counts as a plumbing emergency?

Active leaks, flooding, sewage backups, no water, a leaking water heater, and frozen pipes are emergencies. A dripping faucet or a slow drain in a second bathroom can wait for a regular visit.

What if I smell gas near my water heater?

Leave the house, do not use switches, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside. Once the utility says it is safe, call us to repair the water heater or gas line.
