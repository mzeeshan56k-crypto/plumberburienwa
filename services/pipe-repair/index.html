The basics

What does pipe repair and repiping in Burien include?

Pipe repair includes fixing burst, frozen, cracked, or leaking water lines, and replacing worn-out sections. For homes with failing galvanized or old copper pipe, it can mean a whole-house repipe with PEX or copper. The goal is safe water pressure and no more leaks.

  • Burst pipe repair, day or night
  • Frozen pipe thawing and split pipe replacement
  • Pinhole leak repair in copper
  • Galvanized pipe section replacement
  • Shutoff valve and supply line replacement
  • Whole-house repiping with PEX or copper
  • Pipe insulation for crawl spaces and garages
  • Water pressure testing and pressure regulator service

Warning signs

What are the signs you need pipe repair or repiping?

Look for low water pressure, brown or rusty water, repeat pinhole leaks, and visible rust or green buildup on pipes. Homes built before 1970 with galvanized pipe often show all of these. One leak can be repaired. Many leaks mean the pipes are near the end of their life.

  • Low pressure at several faucets, worse on hot side
  • Brown or rusty water after the tap sits
  • More than one leak in a year
  • Rust, flaking, or bulges on exposed pipe
  • Green or white crust on copper fittings
  • Banging or knocking pipes
  • Gray metal pipe with threaded joints (galvanized)

Pro tip

Before a cold snap, disconnect garden hoses and close crawl space vents. These two steps prevent most frozen pipe calls.

Emergency

What should you do before an emergency plumber arrives for a burst pipe?

Shut off the main water valve right away, then open a low faucet to drain the lines. Turn off the water heater. Move belongings out of the water and call (833) 710-2588. Take photos for your insurer before cleanup. Do not use electrical outlets near standing water.

  1. Shut the main valve where the line enters the house.
  2. Open the lowest faucet to drain pressure.
  3. Turn the water heater to off or pilot.
  4. Switch off breakers for wet areas if safe.
  5. Call a plumber and your insurance company.

Prevention

How do you prevent frozen pipe repairs in Burien?

Most Burien winters are mild, but cold snaps do push temperatures into the teens. Insulate pipes in crawl spaces, garages, and outside walls. Disconnect hoses and cover outdoor faucets. Close crawl space vents during freezes. On the coldest nights, let a faucet on an outside wall drip.

  • Wrap exposed pipes with foam insulation
  • Install frost-free hose bibs on older homes
  • Seal gaps where pipes pass through walls
  • Keep the heat at 55°F or higher if you travel
  • Know where your main shutoff is before it gets cold

Warning

Never thaw a frozen pipe with an open flame. Use a hair dryer and keep the faucet open so melting water can flow.

How it works

How does pipe repair and whole-house repiping work?

For a single leak, the plumber cuts out the bad section and installs new pipe, then tests for leaks. For a repipe, we plan new routes, run new lines through crawl spaces and walls, connect each fixture, and pass inspection. Most repipes take 2 to 5 days, and you usually have water each night.

  2. Plan. Repair a section or repipe, with firm prices for each.
  3. Protect. Cover floors and work areas.
  4. Install. Replace pipe and connect fixtures.
  5. Test and inspect. Pressure test and pass the permit inspection.

Which pipe materials need replacement in Burien homes?

Burien homes built before about 1960 often have galvanized steel supply pipe. Homes from the 1960s to 1990s usually have copper. Newer homes and recent remodels mostly use PEX. Some homes have a mix after partial updates. Knowing your pipe type helps us price the job on the phone.

Repair vs replace

Should you repair a pipe or repipe the whole house?

Repair the pipe if it is your first leak and the rest of the plumbing is in good shape. Repipe if you have galvanized pipe, low pressure throughout, or repeated leaks. Repiping costs more up front, but it ends the cycle of leaks and water damage claims.

FactorRepairRepipe
Pipe typeCopper or PEX in good shapeGalvanized or failing copper

PEX is flexible, resists freezing better than copper, and costs less to install. Copper lasts a long time and some owners prefer it. Burien's soft water is gentle on both. We explain the tradeoffs and price both options so you can choose.

Ask a Plumber Now
How do I know if I have galvanized pipes?

Galvanized pipe is dull gray steel with threaded joints. Scratch it with a key. If it is silver-gray and a magnet sticks, it is galvanized. Copper is reddish brown, and PEX is colored plastic.

Is PEX good for repiping in Burien?

Yes. PEX is approved by code, resists freezing, and works well with Burien's soft water. It is also quicker to install, which lowers labor cost.

How long does a whole-house repipe take?

Most repipes take 2 to 5 days. We usually restore water at the end of each workday.

Can you repair a burst pipe at night?

Yes. We repair burst pipes 24 hours a day. After-hours fees apply, and we tell you the fee on the phone.

Why is my water pressure low?

In older homes, galvanized pipe rusts inside and narrows. Other causes are a failing pressure regulator, a partly closed valve, or a leak. A pressure test finds the cause.

Does repiping require opening walls?

Some small openings are usually needed. In homes with crawl spaces, much of the pipe can be run underneath, which keeps wall openings small.
