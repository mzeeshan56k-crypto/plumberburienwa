How to Flush a Tank Water Heater

Flushing removes the sediment that settles in the bottom of the tank. It helps the heater run quieter, heat faster and last longer.

Water Heaters · November 25, 2026 · 3 min read

Call (833) 710-2588

To flush a water heater, turn off the power or set the gas to pilot, close the cold water supply, attach a garden hose to the drain valve and run it to a safe drain or outside. Open a hot water faucet in the house, then open the drain valve and let the tank empty until the water runs clear. Close the valve, refill the tank fully, and only then turn the heat back on.

Sediment is minerals and grit that settle at the bottom of the tank. Water in the Burien area is generally soft, so sediment builds slower than in many parts of the country. It still collects over time, especially from aging pipes and water main work. Sediment insulates the water from the burner, wastes energy, causes rumbling and can shorten the life of the tank.

How often should you flush?

Once a year is enough for most Burien homes. Fall is a good time, before the heater works hardest in winter. If you hear popping or rumbling, or your hot water runs out sooner than it used to, flush it sooner.

What you need

  • A garden hose long enough to reach a floor drain or the outside
  • A bucket
  • A flathead screwdriver if the drain valve has a slot
  • Gloves

Step by step

  1. Turn off the energy. Gas: turn the control knob to pilot or off. Electric: switch off the breaker.
  2. Let the water cool. Wait a few hours, or overnight, so the water is not scalding.
  3. Close the cold water supply valve on top of the tank.
  4. Attach the hose to the drain valve at the bottom and run it to a drain or downhill outside.
  5. Open a hot water faucet in the house so air can enter and the tank drains.
  6. Open the drain valve. Let the tank drain.
  7. Flush the sediment. When the tank is nearly empty, open the cold supply for a few seconds to stir the sediment, then let it drain. Repeat until the water runs clear.
  8. Close the drain valve and remove the hose.
  9. Refill the tank. Open the cold supply. Leave the hot faucet open until water flows steadily with no air.
  10. Restore the heat. Relight or turn the gas on, or switch the breaker back on. Never turn on an electric heater with an empty tank, or the elements will burn out.

Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
Drain valve will not openDo not force it, call a plumber
Water drains very slowlySediment may be clogging the valve, open and close it gently
Drain valve drips after closingCap it with a hose cap or replace the valve
Tank still rumbles after flushingHeavy hardened sediment, or the tank is near the end of its life

When to call a plumber instead

  • The heater is over 10 years old and has never been flushed. Opening an old drain valve can cause a leak that will not stop.
  • The drain valve is plastic and brittle.
  • You see rust or water around the base.
  • You are not comfortable working with gas controls.

If you see water under the tank, read water heater leaking from the bottom. For an older heater, our guide to water heater lifespan helps you judge how much life it has left.

Flushing tankless heaters

Tankless units need a different flush. A pump circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger. Our guide to tankless water heaters covers the yearly maintenance.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if you never flush your water heater?

Sediment builds up, making the heater less efficient and louder. It can also overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten its life.

Can flushing a water heater cause problems?

On an old heater, opening a crusty drain valve can lead to a leak. If your heater is over 10 years old and has never been flushed, have a plumber do it.

How long does it take to flush a water heater?

About one to two hours including cooling, draining, flushing and refilling.

Do I need to drain the whole tank?

A full drain removes the most sediment. A partial flush of a few gallons every few months also helps.
