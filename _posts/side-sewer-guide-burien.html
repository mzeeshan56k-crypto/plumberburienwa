Side Sewer Guide for Burien Homeowners

The pipe that carries waste from your house to the sewer main is yours to maintain. Here is how it works and what to watch for.

Sewer Lines · November 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Call (833) 710-2588

A side sewer is the private pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the public sewer main, usually under the street. In Burien, the homeowner is generally responsible for the side sewer, including the part under the yard and often up to the connection at the main. When it cracks, clogs with roots or sags, the repair is on the property owner.

Many Burien homes were built from the 1940s through the 1960s. Side sewers from that era were often clay or concrete pipe laid in short sections with joints every few feet. Those joints are where roots get in and where pipes shift. On sloped lots in Seahurst, Three Tree Point and along Marine View Drive SW, side sewers can also run long distances downhill to reach the main. That adds length, cost and access challenges when repairs are needed.

Burien is served by more than one sewer district, mainly Southwest Suburban Sewer District, with Midway and Valley View serving some areas. Each district sets its own rules for connections and repairs. Our guide to Burien sewer districts explains how to find yours.

What are side sewers made of?

  • Clay (vitrified clay). Common before the 1970s. Strong, but joints let roots in and sections crack.
  • Concrete. Found in some older lines. Can erode and crumble over time.
  • Cast iron. Used under some homes and near foundations. Rusts and scales inside.
  • Orangeburg. A fiber pipe used in some mid century homes. It deforms and collapses.
  • PVC or ABS plastic. Used in newer homes and repairs. Joints are glued and resist roots.

Warning signs of a side sewer problem

  • More than one drain is slow at the same time.
  • Toilets gurgle when the washer drains.
  • Water backs up into a basement floor drain or the lowest tub.
  • Sewer smell in the yard or under the house.
  • Soggy patches in the yard along the sewer path.
  • Clogs that come back every few months.

If you see these signs, a camera inspection shows exactly what is happening. Our article on sewer scope inspections explains what the camera finds.

Common side sewer problems in Burien

  1. Root intrusion. Firs, maples and other large trees are common on local lots. Fine roots enter clay joints and grow into thick masses. See tree roots in your sewer line for the full picture.
  2. Offset or broken joints. Soil movement shifts clay sections out of line.
  3. Sags or bellies. A low spot holds water and waste, causing repeat clogs.
  4. Collapse. Older concrete and Orangeburg lines can collapse completely.

Permits for side sewer work

Side sewer repairs and replacements in Burien need a permit, and the sewer district usually inspects the connection. Your plumber should handle the permit. Ask before work starts.

Protect your side sewer

  • Keep grease, wipes and paper towels out of drains.
  • Do not plant new trees over the sewer path.
  • Have the line cleaned on a schedule if roots are a known problem.
  • Install a cleanout if your home does not have one.

Frequently asked questions

Is the side sewer my responsibility in Burien?

In most cases, yes. The homeowner maintains the side sewer from the house to the public main. Confirm the exact responsibility line with your sewer district, since rules differ.

How long does a clay side sewer last?

Clay pipe itself can last 50 to 100 years, but the joints often fail sooner. Roots and soil movement are the usual causes of trouble in lines from the 1940s to 1960s.

Do I need a camera inspection before buying a home?

For any older Burien home, yes. A sewer scope costs a few hundred dollars and can reveal a repair worth thousands before you close.

Can roots be removed for good?

Cutting roots clears the line, but they grow back through the same joints. Lining or replacing the damaged section is the only lasting fix.
