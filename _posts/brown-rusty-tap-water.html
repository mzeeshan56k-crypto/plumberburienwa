Brown or Rusty Tap Water: Causes and Fixes

Brown water from the tap is alarming. The cause can be temporary, like work on the water main, or a sign your pipes or water heater are failing.

Pipes and Water Pressure · December 9, 2026 · 3 min read

Call (833) 710-2588

Brown or rusty tap water is usually caused by rust from old galvanized pipes, a corroding water heater tank, sediment stirred up by water main work or hydrant flushing, or a recent repair in your own plumbing. If it affects only hot water, the water heater is the likely cause. If it affects hot and cold, and especially in the morning, old galvanized pipe is likely. If neighbors see it too, it is probably the water main.

Burien homes get water from Highline Water District or King County Water Districts 20, 49 or 125. Much of the area's supply comes from the Seattle regional system, which delivers clean, generally soft water. When brown water shows up, the problem is usually local: the pipes inside the home, the water heater, or short term disturbances in the street main.

Step by step check

  1. Run the cold water from the tap nearest the main line for a few minutes. If it clears, the problem may be temporary or in your pipes.
  2. Compare hot and cold. Run each separately in a white bucket or sink.
  3. Ask neighbors or check your water district's notices for main work.
  4. Note the timing. Morning only points to pipes. All day points to the source or the heater.

Common causes and fixes

Water main work. District crews flush hydrants and repair mains, which stirs up sediment. Run cold water until it clears. Avoid laundry until then, since rust can stain clothes.

Water heater rust. When the anode rod is used up, the tank starts to rust. Flushing helps briefly, but a rusting tank usually needs replacement. Read water heater lifespan and how to flush a water heater.

Galvanized pipe. Rust flakes break off inside old steel pipe, especially after water sits overnight. This is a sign the pipe is failing. See galvanized pipes in older homes.

After a repair. Turning the water off and on can loosen sediment. Run the taps for a few minutes and clean aerators.

Is brown water safe?

Rust in water is mostly a taste, stain and appearance problem rather than an immediate health risk. But old galvanized pipe can collect lead from upstream sources in some homes. If you have older pipe or brown water that does not clear, test your water and avoid drinking it until you know the cause. Your water district's annual water quality report shows what is in the supply.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my water brown only in the morning?

Water sitting overnight in old galvanized pipe picks up rust. It usually clears after running for a minute. This is a sign the pipe is corroding.

How long does brown water last after main work?

Usually a few hours. Run cold water until it clears. If it lasts more than a day, call your water district.

Can brown water damage my water heater?

Sediment can settle in the tank. Flushing the heater after main work helps.

Should I use a water filter for rusty water?

A filter can reduce rust particles, but it does not fix the cause. Find and fix the source first.
