Hydro Jetting vs Drain Snaking: Which Do You Need?

Both methods clear clogs, but they work differently. The right choice depends on what is in the pipe and what the pipe is made of.

Drains and Clogs · November 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Call (833) 710-2588

Hydro jetting vs snaking comes down to this: a drain snake, or cable machine, cuts a hole through a clog so water can flow again, while hydro jetting uses high pressure water to scour the pipe walls clean. Snaking is faster and cheaper for a single clog. Hydro jetting is better for grease, sludge and roots that keep coming back. Old or cracked pipes may need a snake instead of a jet.

Many Burien homeowners have had a drain snaked, only to see the same clog return a few months later. That usually means the clog was cleared but the buildup on the pipe walls stayed. Kitchen lines with grease and side sewers with roots are the most common examples. Understanding the two methods helps you choose the fix that lasts.

How drain snaking works

A plumber feeds a steel cable into the drain. A motor spins a cutting head on the end. The head breaks up the clog or grabs it so it can be pulled out. Small hand snakes work on sinks and tubs. Larger machines reach main lines and side sewers.

How hydro jetting works

A hose with a special nozzle goes into the pipe. Water at high pressure sprays forward to cut through the clog and backward to push the nozzle along and scrub the walls. Grease, soap scum, scale and fine roots are flushed down to the sewer main.

When snaking is the right choice

  • A one time clog in a sink, tub or toilet.
  • An object stuck in the line.
  • Light roots in a sound pipe, as a quick fix.
  • Older pipe that a camera shows is cracked or fragile.

When hydro jetting is the right choice

  • Kitchen lines with heavy grease. See grease clog prevention.
  • Clogs that come back every few months.
  • Roots in a sewer line that is otherwise sound. Read tree roots in your sewer line.
  • Before a sewer lining job, to prepare the pipe.

Is hydro jetting safe for old pipes?

For most pipes in fair condition, yes. Clay and cast iron lines in older Burien homes can handle jetting if they are not cracked, collapsed or badly offset. A camera inspection first tells you whether the pipe can take it. If the line is fragile, a cable is safer. Learn what the camera shows in sewer scope inspection.

Can you do it yourself?

Hand snakes are fine for small drains. Rental machines for main lines can be dangerous and can get stuck or damage pipe. Hydro jetting equipment is professional only. Pressure washer drain attachments are not the same as real jetting equipment.

What to ask your plumber

  1. Will you camera the line after clearing it?
  2. Is the pipe sound enough for jetting?
  3. How long should this fix last?
  4. Is there a sag or break that will cause it to return?

Frequently asked questions

Does hydro jetting remove tree roots?

It cuts fine and medium roots and flushes them out. Heavy root masses may need a cable cutter first. Roots return if the pipe joints are open.

How often should I hydro jet my drains?

Only when needed. Homes with heavy kitchen use or known roots may benefit every two to three years.

Is hydro jetting worth the extra cost?

For repeat clogs, yes. One jetting often replaces several snaking visits.

Can snaking damage pipes?

A properly sized cable used by a trained plumber is safe. The wrong cutter or a rental machine in the wrong hands can scratch or crack old pipe.
