Water Heater Leaking From the Bottom: What to Do

Water under your water heater can mean a simple valve leak or a tank that is about to fail. Here is how to tell and what to do.

Water Heaters · November 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Call (833) 710-2588

If your water heater is leaking from the bottom, turn off the power or gas to the heater and close the cold water supply valve on top. Then find where the water comes from. A leak from the drain valve or the relief valve pipe can often be repaired. A leak from the tank itself means the tank has rusted through and the water heater must be replaced.

Most tank water heaters in Burien homes sit in a garage, a utility closet or a crawl space. A slow leak can go unnoticed for weeks, especially in a garage. Once a tank starts leaking from the bottom, it can fail suddenly and release 40 or 50 gallons. Acting the day you see water is important.

What to do right away

  1. Turn off the energy. For gas, turn the control to off or pilot. For electric, switch off the breaker.
  2. Close the cold water valve. It is on the pipe going into the top of the tank.
  3. Protect the area. Move stored items and put down towels.
  4. Do not drain a hot tank. Water inside can scald. Let it cool for a few hours if you need to drain it.
  5. Find the source. Use the table below.

Where is the water coming from?

SourceWhat it meansFix
Drain valve at the bottomLoose or worn valveTighten or replace the valve
Relief valve discharge pipePressure or temperature too high, or a bad valveReplace the valve, check pressure
Water on top running downLoose inlet or outlet fittingTighten or replace connectors
CondensationCold water hitting a hot burner, common in winterUsually normal, watch it
Bottom of the tank itselfTank rusted throughReplace the water heater

How to check for a tank leak

Dry the floor and the outside of the tank with towels. Wait and watch. If water appears from under the jacket or from the base with no drip from fittings or valves above, the tank is leaking. Rust streaks on the bottom of the tank are another clue.

Why tanks leak

Over time, the anode rod inside the tank wears away. Once it is gone, the water attacks the steel tank. Sediment at the bottom traps heat and speeds up corrosion. Most tanks start to fail between 8 and 12 years. Our guide to water heater lifespan explains the signs of an aging tank.

Should you repair or replace?

If the leak is from a valve or fitting and the heater is under 10 years old, repair it. If the tank itself leaks, replace it. See repair or replace your water heater for the full decision guide. And if you also lost hot water, no hot water troubleshooting covers other causes.

Frequently asked questions

Is a leaking water heater dangerous?

A leaking tank can fail suddenly and flood the area. Water near a gas burner or electrical parts is also a hazard. Shut off power or gas and the water supply right away.

Can I keep using a water heater that leaks a little?

Not if the tank itself leaks. It will get worse. A small valve leak can wait a short time, but fix it soon.

Why is my water heater leaking from the relief valve?

The valve may be worn, or the tank pressure or temperature may be too high. Thermal expansion without an expansion tank is a common cause.

Does insurance cover a leaking water heater?

Policies may cover sudden water damage, but rarely the heater itself. Document the damage with photos.
