What Size Water Heater Do You Need?

A water heater that is too small runs out of hot water. One that is too large wastes energy. Here is how to size yours right.

Water Heaters · December 15, 2026 · 3 min read

Call (833) 710-2588

The right water heater size depends on how many people live in your home and how much hot water you use at the busiest time of day. As a guide, a 40 gallon tank suits 1 to 3 people, 50 gallons suits 3 to 4 people, and 65 to 80 gallons suits 5 or more. For tankless heaters, size by flow rate in gallons per minute and the temperature rise needed in winter.

Many homeowners in Burien simply replace a water heater with the same size as before. That works if the old one met your needs. But households change. Kids grow up and take longer showers. A new soaking tub or a second bathroom adds demand. Taking a few minutes to size the new heater avoids running out of hot water or paying to heat water you never use.

Tank size by household

1 to 2 people30 to 40 gallons40 to 50 gallons
2 to 3 people40 gallons50 gallons
3 to 4 people40 to 50 gallons50 to 65 gallons
5 or more people50 to 75 gallons65 to 80 gallons

Electric and heat pump tanks recover more slowly than gas, so they are often sized one step larger.

First hour rating

The first hour rating, or FHR, is on the yellow EnergyGuide label. It tells you how many gallons of hot water the heater can deliver in one busy hour. Add up your peak hour use and choose a heater with an FHR that meets or beats it.

Sizing a tankless heater

Tankless units are sized by flow rate and temperature rise.

  1. Add the flow rates of fixtures that run at the same time, such as two showers at 2 gallons per minute each and a kitchen faucet at 1.5.
  2. Find the temperature rise. In the Puget Sound area, incoming water in winter can be in the 40s°F. To reach 120°F, you need a rise of about 70 to 80 degrees.
  3. Choose a unit that delivers your total flow at that rise.

Read tankless water heater pros and cons for more on tankless options.

Common sizing mistakes

  • Choosing a larger tank than needed, which wastes standby energy.
  • Undersizing an electric or heat pump tank.
  • Forgetting a large soaking tub, which can empty a small tank.
  • Sizing a tankless heater for summer water temperatures.

Space and code

Larger tanks need more floor space and height. Gas tanks need proper venting. In Washington, tanks need seismic straps. Heat pump tanks are taller and need air space. See heat pump water heaters.

Cost by size

Larger tanks cost more to buy and install. For local pricing on every type, see water heater replacement cost. If your current heater is struggling, read no hot water troubleshooting first, since the problem may be a part rather than the size.

Frequently asked questions

Is a 40 gallon water heater enough for a family of four?

A 40 gallon gas tank can work for four people with average use. An electric tank of that size may run short, so 50 gallons is safer.

Should I get a bigger water heater if I run out of hot water?

First check for sediment or a failed element. If the heater is healthy and still runs out, a larger tank or a tankless unit may help.

What size tankless water heater do I need?

Most families need a unit that delivers 6 to 9 gallons per minute at a winter temperature rise of about 70 to 80 degrees.

Does a bigger water heater cost more to run?

Yes, slightly. A larger tank loses more heat while standing. Size to your needs, not the biggest available.
